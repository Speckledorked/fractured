# Fractured: Complete System Overview

## What Is Fractured?

Fractured is a narrative-driven tabletop RPG about survival, transformation, and moral weight. Set in a post-outbreak world, characters don't just fight to live—they fight to remain who they are. Under extreme pressure, everyone changes. The only question is: into what?

**Genre**: Survival Horror, Psychological Drama, Post-Apocalyptic
**Players**: 1-4 (solo/co-op/GM-led compatible)
**Campaign Length**: 16-20 sessions (Four Seasons structure)
**Core Mechanic**: 2d6 + stat resolution with narrative outcomes

## Core Systems

### Stats (0-5 scale)
Eight core capabilities:
- **Health**: Physical resilience, injury resistance
- **Stamina**: Endurance, energy under strain
- **Sanity**: Mental resilience, trauma resistance
- **Survival**: Navigation, adaptability, instinct
- **Diplomacy**: Persuasion, empathy, de-escalation
- **Engineering**: Repair, building, improvisation
- **Medical**: Field medicine, triage, diagnosis
- **Combat**: Fighting, tactical awareness, violence

**Starting Distribution**: 16-20 total points across all 8 stats
**Maximum**: 5 (peak human capability)
**Improvement**: Through Growth Marks system

### Resolution System
**Primary**: 2d6 + relevant stat
- **10+**: Success—achieve goal cleanly
- **7-9**: Mixed Success—succeed with cost or complication
- **6-**: Failure—GM makes hard move, situation worsens

**Alternative**: 1d6 for faster, higher-tension play
- **6**: Full success
- **4-5**: Success with cost
- **1-3**: Failure or moral strain

**Modifiers**: ±1 for traits, flaws, conditions, circumstances

### Action Cycle
**No initiative or turn order**—narrative-driven flow:
1. GM frames situation with stakes
2. Players declare actions
3. GM calls for rolls when uncertain
4. Resolve through fiction first, mechanics second
5. GM responds with escalation or new pressure
6. Repeat until Crisis Frame resolves

**Crisis Frames**: Scenes with stakes, pressure, agency, and consequences

### Threat Clocks
Visual escalation trackers:
- **4 segments**: Fast-moving threats (minutes to hours)
- **6 segments**: Building danger (hours to days)
- **8 segments**: Epic threats (campaign-spanning)

**Advance when**: Failed rolls, risky actions, time passes, players hesitate
**When full**: Threat manifests completely—infected breach, ally dies, enemies arrive

## Character Creation

### The 100-Question Quiz
Characters emerge from psychological assessment, not mechanical builds:
- **Section A**: Real-world skills and physical baseline
- **Section B**: Personality and social behavior
- **Section C**: Stress, fear, and morality
- **Section D**: Crisis reactions under pressure
- **Section E**: Psychological texture and hidden drivers

**GM interprets responses** to assign stats, traits, flaws, and Crisis Profile

### Traits
Strengths providing mechanical bonuses and narrative hooks:
- **Types**: Core, Conditional, Latent, Passive, Situational
- **Count**: 2-3 per character
- **Evolution**: Spiral (decay) or Resilience (growth) paths
- **Examples**: Unshakable, Charismatic, Instinctive, Medic, Protector

### Flaws
Vulnerabilities creating complications and depth:
- **Types**: Emotional, Behavioral, Social, Cognitive, Physical, Moral
- **Count**: 1-2 per character
- **Progression**: Worsen (spiral) or resolve (breakthrough)
- **Examples**: Guilt Magnet, Impulse Control, Fear of Failure, Savior Syndrome

### Crisis Profiles
Behavioral archetypes under extreme stress:
- **15 Profiles**: The Fracturer, The Mask, The Fixer, The Ghost, The Martyr, The Anchor, The Firebrand, The Chameleon, The Commander, The Prophet, The Joker, The Wall, The Mirror, The Outsider, The Reclaimer
- **Function**: Guide character behavior during breakdown
- **Effects**: Spiral or Resilience paths, group impact
- **GM Tool**: Not revealed to players unless requested

### Red Lines
Personal moral/emotional boundaries:
- Declared during Session Zero
- "I will never..." format
- Six tags: Identity, Loyalty, Morality, Humanity, Control, Belief
- **Purpose**: Create defining character moments when tested
- **Frequency**: 1-2 times per campaign maximum

## Progression

### Growth Marks System
**Earn Growth Marks** by answering session reflection questions:
1. Did you act according to values—even when costly?
2. Did you meaningfully influence another character?
3. Did you face fear/hardship and grow from it?
4. (Bonus) Did you take personal risk for someone else?

**Each "yes" = 1 Growth Mark**
**3 Growth Marks = 1 Character Advance**

### Character Advances
Choose one:
1. **Stat Improvement**: +1 to any stat (max 5), must be justified
2. **Trait Evolution**: Upgrade existing trait (spiral or resilience path)
3. **Flaw Breakthrough**: Resolve or transform flaw
4. **New Trait**: Gain GM-offered trait based on recent play
5. **Arc Milestone**: Resolve backstory element instead of mechanical bonus

**Campaign Expectation**: 6-10 advances over 16-20 sessions

### Trait Evolution
Traits change under pressure:
- **Triggered by**: 3+ uses in high-stakes situations, Crisis Profile alignment, Red Line moments
- **Player choice**: Spiral (obsessive/harmful) or Resilience (refined/powerful)
- **Examples**:
  - Protector → Blind Guardian (spiral) or Shield of Many (resilience)
  - Charismatic → Manipulator (spiral) or Rallying Flame (resilience)

### Flaw Progression
Flaws intensify or resolve:
- **Spiral**: Triggered repeatedly without addressing, worsens into deeper flaw
- **Breakthrough**: Confronted through roleplay + Sanity roll, transforms into trait or resolves
- **Examples**:
  - Guilt Magnet → Self-Punisher (spiral) or Redeemer (breakthrough)
  - Fear of Failure → Chronic Avoider (spiral) or Unlikely Hero (breakthrough)

## Resource Management

### Seven Categories (0-10 scale)
1. **Medical**: First aid, trauma care, infection control
2. **Ammo**: Ammunition, weapon maintenance
3. **Fuel**: Gas, diesel, propane, generator power
4. **Food/Water**: Hydration, nutrition, ration storage
5. **Utility**: Tools, batteries, radios, rope, flashlights
6. **Sanity/Comfort**: Morale boosts, sentimentals, mental resilience
7. **Construction**: Building materials, fortifications, structural repair

### Resource Flow
**Mission Costs**: Subtract based on team size and mission type
- Solo: -1 per relevant resource
- Small team (2-3): -1 to -2
- Full team (4-6): -2 to -3
- Major operation: -3 to -4+

**Scavenging Gains**: Add based on haul quality
- Minimal: +1
- Standard: +2
- Major: +3
- Exceptional cache: +4 to +5

**Condition-Triggered Drain**: Resources deplete when needs neglected (2+ missions without addressing)

**Sustainability Offsets**: Building systems (farms, workshops, reloading press) reduces drain

### Resource at 0: Crisis
- **Medical**: Infections spread, wounds worsen, no healing
- **Ammo**: Forced into melee/stealth, -2 to combat
- **Fuel**: No movement, generators fail, darkness
- **Food/Water**: Starvation checks, -2 to physical actions
- **Utility**: Failed repairs, blackouts, trap failures
- **Sanity/Comfort**: Morale crash, -1 to Sanity, risk of temporary flaws
- **Construction**: Shelter vulnerable, barricades fail

## Campaign Structure: Four Seasons

### Season 1: The Break (Sessions 1-5)
**Theme**: *"You thought you'd have time. You were wrong."*
- Outbreak begins, world collapses
- First infected encounters
- Establish base, form bonds
- First Red Line moment
- Choose what to carry, who to leave

**Outcomes**: Traumatized but functional, 1-2 advances, group bonded or fractured

### Season 2: The Divide (Sessions 6-10)
**Theme**: *"Now it's just you... and whoever made it through."*
- Resources scarce, rationing begins
- Internal conflicts emerge
- Faction encounters
- Trait evolution opportunities
- Choose who to help, who to turn away

**Outcomes**: Hardened, Crisis Profiles active, 2-3 advances, relationships tested

### Season 3: The Fall (Sessions 11-15)
**Theme**: *"The safe places are gone. So are the safe people."*
- Safe zones destroyed
- Brutal choices required
- Moral compromises deepen
- Red Line proximity constant
- Choose what part of self to kill

**Outcomes**: Transformed or broken, 2-3 advances, Red Lines crossed or held

### Season 4: The Reckoning (Sessions 16-20)
**Theme**: *"You made it here. That doesn't mean you survived it."*
- Final decisions
- Arc resolutions
- Ultimate stakes
- Legacy defined
- Choose who to become

**Outcomes**: Victorious or defeated, arcs complete, permanent transformation

## GM Tools

### GM Moves
**On Mixed Success (7-9)**:
- Escalate danger, extract cost, create urgency, force choice, reveal truth, split attention

**On Failure (6-)**:
- Inflict harm, destroy equipment, drain resources, capture, advance clock, trigger flaw, turn move back, reveal consequence, offer hard choice, create moral complication

### Scene Hooks
15+ ready-to-use tension injectors:
- **Solo prompts**: "The Pack Slows Down for One," "What You Bury Doesn't Stay Buried"
- **Party conflict**: "You Lied to Keep the Peace," "Someone Always Eats First"
- **External threat**: "The Screamer in the Field," "The Child Has a Knife"

### Arc Generators
25+ modular arc scaffolds (1-3 sessions each):
- **Betrayal arcs**: "The One We Left Behind," "The Blood Debt"
- **Collapse arcs**: "The Slow Burn," "The Leadership Vacuum"
- **Discovery arcs**: "The Message That Changes Everything," "The Archive"
- **Sacrifice arcs**: "The Pact," "The One Life for All"
- **Transformation arcs**: "The Reclaimer," "The Ashes of the Lie"

### Red Line Builder
5-step scene template for moral breaking points:
1. **Identify the Line**: What won't they do?
2. **Invert Stakes**: Why breaking it seems necessary
3. **Deny Outs**: Remove alternatives
4. **Frame Moment**: Ask character directly
5. **Echo Aftermath**: Show permanent change

**Includes**: 10+ pre-built scenarios, scene tags, pressure prompts

### Threat Tools
- **Threat Clocks**: 4/6/8 segment escalation trackers
- **Pressure Escalation**: 5-level intensity ladder
- **Multiple Clocks**: Layer 2-3 simultaneous threats
- **Integration**: Connect to resources, Crisis Engine, Red Lines

## Play Examples

### Combat Scene
> **GM**: "Three infected stumble from the darkness, drawn by your flashlights. The pharmacy exit is blocked. What do you do?"
>
> **Player**: "I open fire on the closest one."
>
> **GM**: "Roll 2d6 + Combat."
>
> **Player**: *Rolls 8*
>
> **GM**: "Mixed success. You drop it, but choose one: you're out of ammo now, you drew more infected, or you hit it but didn't stop it."
>
> **Player**: "I'm out of ammo."
>
> **GM**: "Click. Empty. The other two are closing. Ammo resource drops to 0. What do you do?"

### Red Line Moment
> **GM**: "The child—maybe eight years old—is crying in the corner. They saw everything you did. If they tell the others, your group fractures. Maybe violently. They're alone with you. No witnesses. Your 'I won't hurt children' Red Line is being tested. What do you do?"
>
> **Player**: *Long pause* "I... I kneel down. Try to talk to them."
>
> **GM**: "They're terrified of you. Shaking. What do you say?"
>
> **Player**: "I tell them... I tell them I'm sorry they saw that. That we're not bad people. That sometimes... sometimes there's no good choice."
>
> **GM**: "Roll Diplomacy."
>
> **Player**: *Rolls 6*
>
> **GM**: "They scream. Loud. The others hear. They're coming. Your Red Line held—but the cost is immediate."

## Start Playing

**You Need:**
- 2-4 players (including GM)
- 2d6 per player
- Character sheets
- Resource tracker
- Session Zero for character creation

**Session Zero:**
1. Complete 100-question quiz
2. GM assigns stats, traits, flaws
3. Declare Red Lines
4. Establish relationships
5. Set resource starting levels (4-6 each)
6. Frame Season 1 opening

**First Scene:**
Drop characters into action:
- Outbreak is happening NOW
- Immediate choice required
- Show world state through crisis
- Establish tone: tense, personal, consequential

**Then:**
- Follow the fiction
- Make failure interesting
- Track resources
- Build toward Red Lines
- Watch characters transform
