# Resolution System

## Core Philosophy

Fractured uses tension-based minimalism: every roll matters, outcomes shape the narrative, and failure creates story momentum rather than dead ends.

## Primary System: 2d6 + Stat

### When to Roll
Roll **2d6 + relevant stat** when:
- The outcome is uncertain
- Failure has meaningful consequences
- The action fits a stat trigger
- Success isn't guaranteed

### Roll Outcomes

**Total 10+: Success**
- You achieve your goal cleanly
- No complications
- You're in control

**Total 7-9: Mixed Success**
- You succeed, but at a cost
- The GM chooses 1-2 consequences:
  - You draw attention
  - Something breaks, delays, or is exposed
  - You leave yourself or someone else vulnerable
  - You succeed now but pay for it later
  - You must make a hard choice

**Total 6 or less: Failure**
- The action fails—or succeeds in the worst possible way
- The GM makes a hard move:
  - Immediate danger escalation
  - Threat Clock advancement
  - Resource loss
  - Trait/Flaw trigger
  - Moral complication
  - Consequence that snowballs

### Modifiers

| Situation | Modifier |
|-----------|----------|
| Relevant Trait or Training | +1 |
| Significant teammate aid | +1 |
| Active Condition or Flaw | -1 |
| No tools/resources | -1 |
| Severe environmental stress | -1 to -2 |

### Example Resolution

**Scenario:** You're trying to stabilize a bleeding teammate while infected close in.

**Roll:** 2d6 + Medical

**Results:**
- **10+**: You stabilize them quickly and efficiently. They're stable and you have time to move.
- **7-9**: You stabilize them, but choose one:
  - It takes longer than expected—the infected are closer now
  - You use most of your medical supplies
  - They're stable but can't move fast without help
- **6-**: You can't stop the bleeding. They're deteriorating, and the infected have arrived.

## Alternative System: Tension-Based (d6)

For faster, more dramatic play, use single d6 rolls:

### When to Roll
Roll **1d6 (modified by stat if GM allows)** when:
- Speed matters more than granularity
- You want higher tension and risk
- The table prefers simpler math

### Roll Outcomes

**6: Full Success**
- You achieve your goal completely
- No complications

**4-5: Success with Cost**
- You succeed, but something goes wrong
- GM offers a hard choice or complication

**1-3: Failure or Moral Strain**
- The action fails or succeeds badly
- Consequences escalate

### Stat Influence (Optional)
The GM may allow:
- High stat (3-4): +1 to roll
- Low stat (0-1): -1 to roll
- Relevant trait: Reroll once

## GM Moves

### On Mixed Success (7-9 / 4-5)
Choose consequences that create tension:
- **Escalate danger**: The situation worsens
- **Create urgency**: Time becomes critical
- **Force choice**: Pick between two bad options
- **Extract cost**: Resources, trust, or position
- **Reveal truth**: Something hidden emerges
- **Split attention**: Two problems demand response

### On Failure (6- / 1-3)
Make a hard move that changes the situation:
- **Introduce threat**: New danger appears
- **Advance threat clock**: Existing danger worsens
- **Inflict harm**: Injury, exhaustion, trauma
- **Destroy resource**: Equipment, supplies, shelter
- **Break bonds**: Trust fractures, relationships strain
- **Trigger flaw**: Character weakness manifests
- **Reveal consequence**: Past actions catch up
- **Force Red Line**: Moral boundary tested

## Special Roll Types

### Group Rolls
When the party acts together:
- Each player rolls individually
- Take the **middle result** (not highest or lowest)
- This represents group coordination and support
- Alternatively: One player rolls with +1 for each helper (max +3)

### Opposed Rolls
When acting against another character:
- Both sides roll
- Higher total wins
- Tie goes to the defender or status quo
- Margin matters: win by 5+ creates decisive advantage

### Extended Challenges
For complex tasks requiring time:
- Set a target (usually 3-5 successes)
- Each roll represents a stage
- Track successes and complications
- Mixed successes count but add complications
- Three failures = the attempt collapses

**Example: Repairing a vehicle**
- Target: 4 successes
- Success (10+): 1 progress, no complications
- Mixed (7-9): 1 progress, but you need rare parts / it takes longer / something else breaks
- Failure (6-): No progress, and the problem worsens or time runs out

## Combat Resolution

Combat uses the same system but with tactical considerations:

### Combat Actions
- **Attack**: Roll Combat or relevant stat
- **Defend**: Roll Combat or stat to avoid harm
- **Tactical Move**: Roll Survival or Combat to reposition
- **Aid Another**: Roll to provide +1 to ally's next action
- **Desperate Gambit**: Risk everything for major advantage

### Combat Outcomes

**Success (10+):**
- You hit your target effectively
- Enemy is wounded, suppressed, or forced back
- You maintain position

**Mixed (7-9):**
- You hit, but choose one:
  - You expose yourself to counterattack
  - You use extra ammo
  - You cause less damage than intended
  - Collateral damage occurs

**Failure (6-):**
- You miss or are countered
- Enemy gains advantage
- You take damage or lose position

### Narrative Damage
Instead of hit points, track injuries narratively:
- **Minor wounds**: Bleeding, bruised, shaken (-1 to physical actions)
- **Serious wounds**: Broken bones, deep cuts, internal injury (-2 to physical actions, needs medical attention)
- **Critical wounds**: Life-threatening, unconscious, or dying (needs immediate stabilization)

### Death
Death occurs when:
- A critical wound goes untreated
- You choose to sacrifice yourself
- The narrative demands it
- The GM makes it clear death is on the table and you fail

## Sanity and Trauma Rolls

When facing horror, loss, or moral weight:

### Sanity Roll (2d6 + Sanity)
**10+**: You hold steady, process it, stay functional
**7-9**: You cope, but choose one:
- You bottle it (gain temporary flaw)
- You lash out (verbal or physical)
- You withdraw (avoid contact)
- You obsess (can't let it go)
**6-**: You break. The GM chooses:
- Panic response (freeze, flee, fight)
- Shutdown (dissociate, go numb)
- Spiral (existing flaw worsens)
- Crisis Profile triggers

### Trauma Accumulation
Failed Sanity rolls can lead to:
- New flaws developing
- Existing flaws intensifying
- Crisis Profile shifts
- Red Line moments emerging

## Resource and Survival Rolls

When scavenging, rationing, or managing supplies:

### Scavenging (2d6 + Survival)
**10+**: Excellent haul (+3 to relevant resource)
**7-9**: Good find (+2), but choose one:
- It takes longer than expected
- You had to go somewhere dangerous
- You attracted attention
**6-**: Poor haul (+1) and trouble follows

### Rationing (2d6 + relevant stat)
**10+**: Supplies stretched successfully, no penalties
**7-9**: Stretched but strained—temporary -1 to morale
**6-**: Rationing fails—resource depletes and group tension rises

## Recovery Rolls

### Medical Recovery (2d6 + Medical)
Rolled when treating wounds or illness:
**10+**: Full recovery, wound healed or stabilized
**7-9**: Partial recovery, but:
- Takes longer
- Requires extra supplies
- Leaves a complication (scar, weakness)
**6-**: Condition worsens or infection sets in

### Sanity Recovery (2d6 + Sanity)
Rolled during downtime when processing trauma:
**10+**: You process it healthily, reduce trauma
**7-9**: You cope, but it lingers
**6-**: You can't process it—trauma deepens

## Rolling with Advantage/Disadvantage

### Advantage (Roll 3d6, keep highest 2)
Granted when:
- Ideal conditions align
- Perfect tools are available
- Expert help is present
- Trait strongly applies

### Disadvantage (Roll 3d6, keep lowest 2)
Applied when:
- Multiple factors work against you
- Severe conditions hamper you
- Active flaw strongly applies
- You're working against time and pressure

## When NOT to Roll

Don't roll when:
- **Outcome is certain**: Automatic success or failure makes more sense
- **No stakes exist**: Rolling doesn't add tension
- **It slows the story**: Narrative flow matters more
- **Competence is assumed**: A trained medic bandaging a minor cut
- **Failure has no interesting outcome**: Dead ends aren't fun

## GM Guidelines

### Setting Difficulty
Don't add flat difficulty numbers. Instead:
- **Easy tasks**: No roll needed, automatic success
- **Standard tasks**: Base 2d6 + stat
- **Hard tasks**: Apply -1 modifier
- **Nearly impossible**: Apply -2 modifier or require creative approach

### Interpreting Results
Let the fiction guide outcomes:
- Consider the character's approach
- Factor in the environment and situation
- Think about consequences beyond success/failure
- Look for opportunities to escalate tension
- Use failures to drive story forward, not stop it

### Pacing Rolls
Not every action needs a roll:
- **Montage actions**: Describe success, move on
- **Preparatory actions**: Set up advantages for the roll that matters
- **Character moments**: Let roleplay resolve without dice
- **Downtime**: Assume competence unless crisis emerges

### Failed Rolls Create Story
When a player fails:
- Don't just say "you fail"
- Show what goes wrong
- Introduce new complications
- Advance threat clocks
- Trigger consequences
- Create new choices

Failure is never the end—it's the next chapter.
