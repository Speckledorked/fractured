# Core Stats System

## Philosophy

Stats in Fractured are narrative triggers, not hard math. They represent a character's capability and instinct in crisis situations. When you do something risky that fits a core stat, you roll—and the outcome shapes the story.

## The Eight Stats

Each stat is rated from 0 to 5:
- **0-1**: Weak, untrained, or actively detrimental
- **2**: Average, functional with support
- **3**: Confident, skilled, trained
- **4**: Exceptional, hardened, professional
- **5**: Peak human capability (rare)

### Health
**Physical resilience, resistance to injury, recovery rate**

Roll Health when:
- You need to shrug off injury or physical resistance
- You're recovering from wounds or illness
- You're resisting environmental damage (cold, heat, poison)

### Stamina
**Endurance, energy, ability to perform long tasks under pressure**

Roll Stamina when:
- You push your body past safe limits
- You've been awake too long or working without rest
- You need to perform extended physical activity
- You refuse rest to keep someone else alive

**Example triggers:**
- Sprinting with someone on your back while being chased
- Taking another shift after 36 hours awake
- Carrying heavy equipment uphill
- Refusing medical rest

### Sanity
**Mental resilience, resistance to fear/trauma, emotional regulation**

Roll Sanity when:
- You face horror, grief, or the unnatural
- You witness something mentally shattering
- You must keep composure under psychological stress
- You're confronted with moral or existential weight

**Example triggers:**
- Discovering a loved one turned
- Being the only one awake during a long watch, hearing whispers
- A decision you made costs someone their life
- Seeing something impossible or terrifying

### Survival
**Navigation, foraging, adaptability to harsh conditions**

Roll Survival when:
- You act on instinct in dangerous terrain or unknown conditions
- You need to navigate, find shelter, or locate resources
- You're adapting to wilderness or hostile environments

**Example triggers:**
- Crawling through a collapsed tunnel
- Foraging at night in unknown territory
- Bypassing frozen water without testing it
- Eating something of questionable safety when desperate

### Diplomacy
**Persuasion, negotiation, empathy, conflict de-escalation**

Roll Diplomacy when:
- You try to persuade, negotiate, or influence others
- You attempt to de-escalate conflict or calm tensions
- You're building trust or reading social situations

**Example triggers:**
- Negotiating with hostile survivors
- Calming a panicking teammate
- Building trust with a suspicious faction
- Mediating between conflicting group members

### Engineering
**Ability to repair, build, modify tools, machines, structures**

Roll Engineering when:
- You repair or modify equipment
- You build structures or fortifications
- You work with complex mechanical or electrical systems
- You improvise solutions with limited materials

**Example triggers:**
- Repairing a vehicle under pressure
- Jury-rigging a generator
- Building fortifications
- Modifying weapons or equipment

### Medical
**Field medicine, diagnosis, crisis triage, long-term care**

Roll Medical when:
- You treat wounds or stabilize the dying
- You diagnose illness or infection
- You perform surgery or complex medical procedures
- You manage long-term patient care

**Example triggers:**
- Field triage after combat
- Treating infection without proper supplies
- Emergency surgery
- Diagnosing mysterious symptoms

### Combat
**Firearms, melee, tactical awareness, reaction time**

Roll Combat when:
- You engage in armed or unarmed conflict
- You need tactical awareness in dangerous situations
- You defend yourself or others from threats
- You react quickly to sudden violence

**Example triggers:**
- Firefights with hostiles
- Hand-to-hand combat with infected
- Tactical positioning during ambush
- Quick-draw situations

## Stat Interactions

### Overlapping Stats
Sometimes multiple stats could apply. The GM chooses based on the approach:
- Sneaking past infected: **Survival** (instinct) vs **Combat** (tactical awareness)
- Treating a panicking patient: **Medical** (technique) vs **Diplomacy** (calming them first)
- Building under pressure: **Engineering** (skill) vs **Stamina** (endurance)

### Synergy
High stats in related areas provide narrative advantages:
- High **Medical** + High **Diplomacy** = trusted healer
- High **Combat** + High **Survival** = dangerous scout
- High **Engineering** + High **Stamina** = tireless builder

### Weakness
Low stats create vulnerability and roleplay opportunities:
- Low **Sanity** but high **Combat** = effective but fragile
- Low **Health** but high **Medical** = wounded healer archetype
- Low **Diplomacy** but high **Survival** = lone wolf survivor

## Edge Cases

### Physically weak, mentally unbreakable
Health 0-1, Sanity 4-5: Frail body, iron will

### Skilled but unstable
High technical stats (Engineering, Medical) but low Sanity: Capable when focused, dangerous when stressed

### Charismatic coward
High Diplomacy, low Combat/Stamina: Talks well, runs often

## Using Stats in Play

### When to Roll
The GM calls for a roll when:
- The outcome is uncertain
- Failure has consequences
- The action fits a stat trigger
- The moment has dramatic weight

### When NOT to Roll
Don't roll when:
- The outcome is certain (automatic success or failure)
- There are no stakes
- The action doesn't fit any stat
- Rolling would slow the narrative

### Modifiers
Stats can be modified by:
- **Traits** (+1 when applicable)
- **Flaws** (-1 when triggered)
- **Conditions** (-1 to -2 for exhaustion, injury, stress)
- **Equipment** (+1 for proper tools)
- **Help** (+1 for significant teammate aid)
- **Environment** (-1 to -2 for harsh conditions)

### Assignment Guidelines

**Response Pattern → Point Value:**
- Weak/passive/detrimental: **0-1 points**
- Average/functional: **2 points**
- Confident/skilled: **3 points**
- Exceptional/hardened: **4 points**
- Peak capability: **5 points** (rare)

### Starting Distribution
Most characters start with:
- Total of 16-20 stat points distributed across all 8 stats
- No stat above 4 at creation (5 is earned through play)
- At least one stat at 0-1 (weakness creates story)
- 2-3 stats at 3-4 (areas of competence)

## Stat Improvement

Stats can improve through the **Growth Marks** system:

**Every 3 Growth Marks**, a character may:
- Raise one stat by +1 (max 5)
- Improvement must be justified by recent in-game experience
- You cannot improve a stat you haven't used meaningfully

**Example justifications:**
- Raise Combat after surviving multiple firefights
- Raise Medical after treating serious injuries
- Raise Sanity after enduring major trauma and processing it
- Raise Engineering after successful building projects

## GM Notes

### Stat as Character
A character's stat array tells their story:
- A medic with low Combat has made different choices than a soldier with low Medical
- High Survival + Low Diplomacy suggests a lone wolf background
- High Sanity + Low Health might indicate someone who's suffered physically but stayed mentally whole

### Stat-Driven Roleplay
Use stats to prompt character moments:
- Low Stamina character is always exhausted—when do they push anyway?
- High Diplomacy character can usually talk their way out—what happens when words fail?
- Low Sanity character is fragile—how do they hide it from others?

### Balance
Don't worry about "balanced" stat arrays. Weaknesses create drama. Strengths create competence. Both are valuable.
