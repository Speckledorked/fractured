# Crisis Engine

## Overview

The Crisis Engine tracks how characters change under pressure. It transforms traits into evolution paths, flaws into defining moments, and stress into permanent character development.

## Core Concept

**Under pressure, people don't stay the same—they evolve or fracture.**

The Crisis Engine ensures that:
- Prolonged stress changes characters
- Traits evolve through use and testing
- Flaws intensify or resolve
- Crisis Profiles emerge and shift
- No character ends the story as they began

## Crisis Tags

Every trait and flaw carries hidden potential that emerges under stress.

### Tag Types

**🔺 Stress Reactive**
The trait or flaw mutates under emotional or situational pressure.
- Becomes more pronounced when triggered repeatedly
- May shift into related trait/flaw
- Creates feedback loops with Crisis Profiles

**⚖️ Group Modifier**
Impacts team morale, cohesion, or leadership dynamics.
- Affects how others perceive the character
- Can stabilize or destabilize the group
- Influences NPC reactions

**🕳 Spiral Risk**
May degrade into worse behaviors if ignored.
- Requires intervention or recovery to prevent
- Can lead to permanent character shifts
- Often creates Red Line moments

**🌱 Resilience Arc**
Can evolve into a powerful strength after being tested.
- Requires successful navigation of crisis
- Often emerges after Red Line moments
- Creates lasting character growth

### How Tags Work

Tags aren't visible to players initially—they emerge through play:

**Example: "Protector" Trait**
- Core: +1 when defending others
- Tags: 🔺 (becomes possessive under stress), 🌱 (can evolve into "Shield of Many")
- Spiral Path: "Blind Guardian" (sacrifices judgment for protection)
- Resilience Path: "Guardian Angel" (protects wisely and selflessly)

The GM tracks tags and triggers them when pressure mounts.

## Stress Accumulation

Characters accumulate stress through:
- **Failed Sanity rolls**
- **Prolonged resource scarcity**
- **Repeated moral compromises**
- **Loss of companions**
- **Physical exhaustion**
- **Red Line proximity**

### Stress Thresholds

**Low Stress (0-2 failures/events)**
- Normal functioning
- Traits work as expected
- Flaws are manageable

**Moderate Stress (3-4 failures/events)**
- Traits begin to intensify
- Flaws trigger more frequently
- Crisis Profile begins emerging

**High Stress (5-6 failures/events)**
- Evolution paths become visible
- Must choose: Spiral or Resilience
- Crisis Profile fully active

**Critical Stress (7+ failures/events)**
- Automatic trait/flaw mutation
- Red Line moments imminent
- Permanent character change

### Tracking Stress

The GM can track stress:
- **Formally**: Mark stress boxes (1-10 scale)
- **Informally**: Note failed Sanity rolls and traumatic events
- **Narratively**: Watch for character changes in roleplay

Choose the method that fits your table.

## Trait Evolution

### Evolution Triggers

Traits evolve when:
- Used successfully in high-stakes situations (3+ times)
- Tested during Crisis Frame with related Crisis Profile
- Character survives Red Line moment related to trait
- GM determines enough time/pressure has passed

### Evolution Process

**1. Identify the Trait**
Which trait has been most prominent or tested?

**2. Determine Direction**
Is the character spiraling or showing resilience?
- **Spiral**: Trait becomes obsessive, rigid, or harmful
- **Resilience**: Trait becomes refined, powerful, or transcendent

**3. Offer Choice**
Present the evolution to the player:
> "Your 'Protector' trait has been tested repeatedly. You can evolve it into:
> - **Spiral**: 'Blind Guardian' – You protect without judgment, even the unworthy
> - **Resilience**: 'Shield of Many' – You protect with wisdom and sacrifice"

**4. Apply Evolution**
Replace the old trait with the evolved version
- New mechanical effect
- New narrative trigger
- New evolution paths (traits can evolve multiple times)

### Evolution Examples

**"Charismatic" → Evolutions**
- Spiral: "Manipulator" (uses charm to control)
- Resilience: "Rallying Flame" (inspires genuine hope)

**"Calculated Risk-Taker" → Evolutions**
- Spiral: "Broken Gambler" (can't stop risking)
- Resilience: "Heroic Operator" (risks with precision)

**"Empathic Speaker" → Evolutions**
- Spiral: "Compassion Fatigue" (absorbs too much pain)
- Resilience: "Emotional Linchpin" (holds group together)

## Flaw Progression

### Flaw Spirals

Flaws worsen when:
- Triggered repeatedly without addressing them
- Character reaches high stress
- Related Crisis Profile activates
- Character fails to process trauma

### Spiral Process

**1. Initial Flaw**
Example: "Guilt Magnet"
- Effect: Absorbs responsibility even when not theirs
- Trigger: When others are lost or hurt

**2. First Spiral Warning**
After 2-3 triggers:
> "Your guilt is starting to consume you. You can't sleep. You see their faces when you close your eyes."
- No mechanical change yet
- Narrative weight increases

**3. Second Spiral Warning**
After 4-5 triggers:
> "You're drowning in guilt. Make a choice:
> - **Confront it**: Roll Sanity to process and potentially resolve the flaw
> - **Suppress it**: Let it spiral into 'Self-Punisher'"

**4. Full Spiral**
If unaddressed:
- Flaw becomes "Self-Punisher"
- New effect: Actively seeks punishment, refuses help
- Creates Red Line moments

### Flaw Breakthrough

Flaws can be resolved through:
- **Confronting the source** (roleplay + Sanity roll)
- **Earning 3 Growth Marks** and choosing Breakthrough as advance
- **Red Line moment** that forces reckoning
- **Sustained support** from group over multiple sessions

### Breakthrough Process

**1. Create the Moment**
Frame a scene where the character confronts their flaw:
- Face the person they wronged
- Revisit the trauma source
- Make opposite choice to their flaw
- Accept forgiveness or responsibility

**2. Roll for It**
Make a Sanity or relevant stat roll:
- **10+**: Breakthrough—flaw resolved or transformed
- **7-9**: Progress—flaw reduces intensity, may need one more moment
- **6-**: Spiral—flaw worsens or mutates

**3. Apply Transformation**
If breakthrough succeeds:
- Flaw is removed or becomes lesser version
- Character gains new trait reflecting growth
- Permanently changes Crisis Profile

**Example:**
"Guilt Magnet" breakthrough → becomes "Redeemer" trait
- Effect: +1 when helping others face consequences
- Narrative: You've processed your guilt and use it to help others

## Crisis Profiles

Crisis Profiles are **behavioral archetypes under stress**—they define who you become when everything falls apart.

### The 15 Profiles

1. **The Fracturer**: Emotional chaos, outbursts, blame-shifting
2. **The Mask**: Emotional concealment, hidden collapse
3. **The Fixer**: Control through action, hyper-tasking
4. **The Ghost**: Withdrawal, isolation, disappearance
5. **The Martyr**: Self-sacrifice, volunteers for all risks
6. **The Anchor**: Stabilization, grounds others, internalizes stress
7. **The Firebrand**: Confrontation, demands action/justice
8. **The Chameleon**: Adaptive masking, identity loss
9. **The Commander**: Authoritarian control, demands obedience
10. **The Prophet**: Symbolic meaning, claims visions
11. **The Joker**: Deflection through humor, masks fear
12. **The Wall**: Hardened shutdown, becomes pure function
13. **The Mirror**: Emotional absorption, reflects group state
14. **The Outsider**: Distrust, assumes betrayal
15. **The Reclaimer**: Transformation through pain, rebuilds identity

### Profile Activation

Profiles emerge when:
- Character reaches moderate stress
- Specific triggers align with profile
- GM determines it fits the character's arc

### Profile Effects

**Primary Response**
How the character instinctively acts under pressure
- Example (The Anchor): Speaks calmly, focuses group, uses grounding body language

**Secondary Response**
What happens if the pressure continues
- Example (The Anchor): Internalizes stress, denies personal needs, burns out slowly

**Behavioral Signs**
GM cues for portraying the profile
- Example (The Anchor): "You find yourself putting a hand on their shoulder. You use a calm voice even though you want to scream."

**Spiral Path**
What happens if things get worse
- Example (The Anchor): Burns out completely, may suffer breakdown when alone or post-crisis

**Resilience Path**
What happens if the character navigates it well
- Example (The Anchor): Evolves into spiritual or emotional leader—respected, centered

**Group Effect**
How the profile affects others
- Example (The Anchor): Provides cohesion, boosts morale under pressure, but their collapse is catastrophic

### Using Profiles

**As GM:**
1. Watch character behavior under stress
2. Identify which profile is emerging
3. Use behavioral signs to prompt roleplay
4. Track spiral vs resilience direction
5. Create scenes that test or support the profile

**Don't:**
- Tell players their profile (keep it GM-only unless they ask)
- Force behaviors that contradict player choices
- Use profiles as straitjackets—they're guides, not rules

## Stress Response Cycles

### Recognition
When a character shows stress:
1. **Identify trigger**: What caused the stress?
2. **Note response**: How did they react?
3. **Track pattern**: Is this consistent with past behavior?
4. **Assign profile**: Does a Crisis Profile fit?

### Intervention
Other characters can help:
- **Empathy roll**: Try to talk them down
- **Group support**: Spend downtime helping them process
- **Remove trigger**: Change situation to reduce stress
- **Force rest**: Make them take time to recover

### Resolution
Stress resolves through:
- **Successful processing**: Sanity recovery roll
- **Trait evolution**: Channel stress into growth
- **Flaw breakthrough**: Confront and overcome
- **Red Line reckoning**: Major character moment

### Escalation
If unresolved:
- Stress accumulates
- Profile deepens
- Flaws spiral
- Red Line approaches

## Group Dynamics

### Stabilizers
Some characters stabilize others:
- **The Anchor profile** reduces others' stress
- **High Diplomacy** can talk people down
- **"Protective Presence" trait** boosts group morale

### Amplifiers
Some characters escalate stress:
- **The Fracturer profile** spreads emotional chaos
- **Certain flaws** (like "Emotional Volatility") are contagious
- **Low Sanity** characters can panic others

### Feedback Loops
Watch for:
- **Positive loops**: Anchor + Protector = stable group
- **Negative loops**: Fracturer + Mirror = group meltdown
- **Volatile mixes**: Firebrand + Commander = power struggle

### Managing Group Stress

**As GM:**
- Track which characters stabilize and which amplify
- Create scenes where stabilizers can act
- Show consequences when stabilizers break
- Allow groups to develop coping mechanisms
- Reward healthy group dynamics

## Crisis Engine in Play

### Session Start
Check each character's:
- Current stress level
- Active Crisis Profile
- Traits on evolution track
- Flaws at risk of spiraling

### During Session
Watch for:
- Failed Sanity rolls (add stress)
- Trait triggers (count toward evolution)
- Flaw activations (track spiral risk)
- Profile-consistent behavior (reinforce it)

### Session End
Update:
- Stress levels based on events
- Trait evolution progress
- Flaw spiral warnings
- Crisis Profile shifts

### Between Sessions
Consider:
- Which characters are ready for evolution
- Which flaws need breakthrough moments
- Which profiles need testing
- What Red Line moments are emerging

## Advanced Techniques

### Compounding Crises
Layer multiple pressures:
- Resource scarcity + moral dilemma + physical threat
- Forces characters to show true nature
- Accelerates trait evolution and flaw spirals

### Mirror Moments
Create scenes where characters see themselves:
- Encounter NPC who represents their spiral path
- Face consequence of their flaw
- Watch ally embody their resilience path
- Creates metacognitive growth

### Pressure Release Valves
Provide safe outlets:
- Downtime for processing
- Supportive NPCs who listen
- Moments of beauty or peace
- Small victories and progress

### Inevitable Evolution
Every character must change:
- By Season 2: One trait evolved or one flaw spiraling
- By Season 3: Crisis Profile fully active
- By Season 4: Red Line crossed or major breakthrough

No one survives unchanged.

## GM Principles

### Respect Player Agency
- Offer choices in evolution
- Telegraph consequences
- Allow players to direct growth
- Don't force specific outcomes

### Make Change Meaningful
- Evolution should feel earned
- Spirals should feel tragic but inevitable
- Breakthroughs should feel cathartic
- Profiles should emerge naturally

### Track, Don't Prescribe
- Watch what characters do
- Note patterns and themes
- Offer evolution when ready
- Let characters surprise you

### Use Pressure Wisely
- Too much: overwhelms and exhausts
- Too little: stagnates and bores
- Just right: creates growth and story

The Crisis Engine turns survivors into characters—flawed, complex, and unforgettable.
