# GM Moves and Principles

## Overview

GM Moves are your toolkit for responding to player actions, especially when they roll mixed success (7-9) or failure (6-). They keep the story moving, escalate tension, and ensure failure creates story momentum.

---

## Core GM Principles

### 1. Follow the Fiction
Let the narrative guide mechanics, not the other way around. If the fiction says the infected break through, they break through—regardless of what you planned.

### 2. Make Failure Interesting
Never let failure be a dead end. Failed rolls create complications, escalate danger, or force new choices.

### 3. Telegraph Danger
Make risks clear before calling for rolls. Players should know what's at stake.

### 4. Honor Player Agency
Let bold plans work. Let creative solutions succeed. Respect character choices.

### 5. Ask Questions
Use questions to build the world, develop characters, and create investment.

### 6. Be a Fan of the Characters
Root for them, even as you challenge them. Make their struggles matter.

### 7. Think Off-Screen
The world continues when players aren't looking. Threats evolve, NPCs act, situations change.

### 8. Embrace the Chaos
Don't fight unexpected player choices—follow them and see where they lead.

---

## When to Make Moves

### On Mixed Success (7-9 / 4-5)
Player succeeds but with complication. Choose one:
- Reduced effect
- Complication
- Cost or consequence
- Hard choice

### On Failure (6- / 1-3)
Player fails or succeeds in worst way. Make a hard move:
- Escalate danger immediately
- Inflict harm or loss
- Advance threat clocks
- Force impossible choice
- Trigger flaw or crisis

### When Players Look to You
- They hesitate or stall
- They ask "what do we see?"
- They finish actions and wait
- Conversation lulls

### When There's a Golden Opportunity
- Perfect moment for complication
- Threat clock about to fill
- Red Line proximity
- Dramatic timing

## GM Moves: Mixed Success

Use these when players roll 7-9:

### Escalate Danger
Make situation more urgent or complicated.

**Examples:**
- "You stabilize her, but infected heard the commotion—they're coming."
- "You pick the lock, but the noise echoes. Someone knows you're here."
- "You convince him, but his partner doesn't trust you."

### Extract Cost
Success comes with price.

**Examples:**
- "You make the shot, but you're out of ammo now."
- "You patch the wound, but you used the last bandages."
- "You outrun them, but you're exhausted—Stamina check."

### Create Urgency
Add time pressure.

**Examples:**
- "You repair the generator, but it won't last long—maybe an hour."
- "You convince her to wait, but not forever. She's leaving at dawn."
- "You find the supplies, but the building is collapsing around you."

### Force Choice
Make them choose between two important things.

**Examples:**
- "You can save the supplies or grab the medical gear—not both."
- "You convince one faction, but alienate the other."
- "You stop the bleeding, but the noise will draw infected."

### Reveal Unwelcome Truth
Success brings uncomfortable information.

**Examples:**
- "You hack the terminal. Bad news: the safe zone is overrun."
- "You recognize them—they're the ones who left you to die."
- "You translate the journal. It describes experiments... on people."

### Split Attention
Create secondary problem.

**Examples:**
- "You barricade the door, but now you hear breaking glass upstairs."
- "You calm Marcus down, but Sarah's slipping away unnoticed."
- "You negotiate successfully, but your team looks betrayed."

## GM Moves: Failure

Use these when players roll 6- (or 1-3):

### Hard Moves

**Inflict Harm**
Cause direct physical damage.
- "The infected tackles you—take serious wound to shoulder."
- "The floor gives way. You fall through to the basement."
- "He pulls a knife. Roll Combat or take critical injury."

**Destroy Equipment**
Break or lose important items.
- "Your weapon jams—permanently. It's scrap now."
- "The radio shatters when you drop it."
- "The generator explodes, showering sparks everywhere."

**Drain Resources**
Deplete a resource category.
- "You waste medical supplies trying—Medical -2."
- "The firefight burns through ammo—Ammo -3."
- "Three days of rations ruined by water damage."

**Capture or Separate**
Split party or trap character.
- "They grab you. You're being dragged away."
- "The door slams shut between you and your team."
- "You're surrounded. Hands up or die."

**Advance Threat Clock**
Push danger closer.
- "The infected are through the first barricade. [Clock advances 2]"
- "She's bleeding out faster. [Clock advances 2]"
- "Their reinforcements arrive. [Clock advances 3]"

**Trigger Flaw or Crisis**
Activate character weakness.
- "Your 'Guilt Magnet' flares. You freeze, remembering..."
- "Your Crisis Profile activates—you're spiraling."
- "Your fear of tight spaces overwhelms you. Sanity check."

**Turn Move Back on Them**
Their action creates opposite effect.

- "You try to sneak past—they hear you. Roll Combat as they open fire."
- "You reach for the cure—it slips and shatters."
- "You lie to protect them—she sees through it and walks out."

**Reveal Consequence**
Past actions catch up.

- "That survivor you turned away? He's with the raiders now. And he remembers you."
- "The wound you ignored is infected. Make Medical check or it spreads."
- "Your lie unravels—everyone knows the truth now."

**Offer Hard Choice**
Force immediate decision under pressure.

- "Infected are coming. You can grab supplies or help him. Choose now."
- "She's dying. You can try to save her or escape. Ten seconds."
- "They demand one of you stays behind. Who?"

**Create Moral Complication**
Add ethical weight.

- "You kill them. Then you realize: they were trying to surrender."
- "The supplies are there—guarded by starving children."
- "You win the firefight. Survivors are now hostile to all outsiders because of you."

## Move Examples by Situation

### Combat Situations

**Mixed Success:**
- Hit but expose yourself to counterattack
- Kill but use last ammo
- Dodge but lose position
- Wound but don't stop them

**Failure:**
- Take serious injury
- Weapon jams or breaks
- Allies get hurt protecting you
- Tactical disadvantage created

### Social Situations

**Mixed Success:**
- Convince but create obligation
- Calm but don't resolve
- Negotiate but give too much
- Trust formed but fragile

**Failure:**
- Relationship damaged
- Lie exposed
- Threat escalates to violence
- NPC turns hostile

### Survival Situations

**Mixed Success:**
- Find resources but attract attention
- Navigate but take longer
- Repair but temporary
- Shelter but vulnerable

**Failure:**
- Lost in dangerous territory
- Resources destroyed
- Shelter compromised
- Exposure to elements

### Medical Situations

**Mixed Success:**
- Stabilize but use critical supplies
- Heal but leaves complication
- Diagnose but treatment unavailable
- Save but they're changed

**Failure:**
- Condition worsens
- Infection spreads
- Patient dies despite efforts
- Medical supplies wasted

## Asking Questions

Use questions to build world and character:

### World-Building Questions
- "What did this place look like before?"
- "Who do you think lived here?"
- "What's the worst thing that could be in there?"

### Character Questions
- "What does this remind you of?"
- "Who taught you that?"
- "What are you afraid they'll find out?"
- "When was the last time you felt this way?"

### Pressure Questions
- "What do you do?"
- "How does that work?"
- "Are you sure?"
- "What are you hoping for?"

### Relationship Questions
- "How do you look at them after that?"
- "What do you want to say but don't?"
- "Do they notice you watching?"

## Building Scenes

### Scene Opening
- Establish location clearly
- State what's immediately apparent
- Indicate time of day/weather
- Present initial tension or calm

**Example:**
> "You're in what used to be a grocery store. Shelves toppled, glass everywhere. It's midday but dark inside. You hear dripping water—or something else."

### Scene Escalation
- Introduce complication
- Advance threat clock
- Create decision point
- Raise stakes

**Example:**
> "As you search the pharmacy section, you hear it—shuffling from the back room. Then another sound joins it. And another."

### Scene Climax
- Force action
- Make threats real
- Demand choices
- Resolve tension

**Example:**
> "Three infected stumble from the back, drawn by your flashlights. You're cornered between them and the blocked entrance. What do you do?"

### Scene Resolution
- Show consequences
- Allow breathing room
- Connect to larger story
- Set up next scene

**Example:**
> "You escape through the roof access, Medical supplies in hand. But Marcus took a bite to the forearm. He's bleeding. The clock starts now."

## Threat Clock Management

### Starting Clocks
- Begin clock when threat is introduced
- Make some clocks visible to players
- Keep others hidden for tension
- Use 4-segment for fast threats, 6-segment for slow

### Advancing Clocks
Advance when:
- Players fail relevant roll
- Players take noisy/risky action
- Time passes narratively
- Players hesitate or stall

**Don't advance every failed roll**—use judgment for pacing.

### Multiple Clocks
Layer pressures:
- Breach clock (infected breaking in)
- Bleeding clock (ally dying)
- Discovery clock (enemies searching)

Forces players to prioritize.

### Filled Clocks
When clock fills, threat manifests:
- Describe it cinematically
- Make it impactful
- Follow through completely
- Create new pressure

**Don't pull punches when clocks fill.**

## GM Moves for Different Play Styles

### For Tactical Players
- Provide clear threat information
- Reward smart planning
- Make consequences logical
- Allow clever solutions

### For Roleplayers
- Focus on character moments
- Use emotional pressure
- Create relationship tension
- Honor character choices

### For Story Seekers
- Advance arcs actively
- Create dramatic moments
- Connect scenes to themes
- Build toward climax

### For Challenge Seekers
- Increase danger
- Limit resources
- Force hard choices
- Make threats real

**Adapt to your table.**

## Common GM Mistakes

### Over-Planning
**Problem**: Scripted scenes feel railroaded
**Solution**: Frame situations, not outcomes. Follow player choices.

### Soft Failure
**Problem**: Failures have no teeth, rolls don't matter
**Solution**: Make failures hurt. Escalate. Advance clocks.

### Hidden Stakes
**Problem**: Players don't know what they're risking
**Solution**: Telegraph danger. State stakes before rolls.

### Ignoring Fiction
**Problem**: Mechanics override narrative sense
**Solution**: Let fiction guide. If it makes sense, it happens.

### Pulling Punches
**Problem**: Protecting characters from consequences
**Solution**: Honor the danger. Let threats be real.

### Analysis Paralysis
**Problem**: Overthinking every move
**Solution**: Trust instincts. Make a move. Keep it flowing.

## Advanced Techniques

### The Double-Move
On hard failure, make two moves:
- Immediate harm + advance threat clock
- Destroy equipment + reveal complication

**Use sparingly—very high impact.**

### The Quiet Move
Make off-screen move without announcing:
- NPCs act while players focus elsewhere
- Threats evolve in background
- Consequences build unseen

**Reveal later for maximum effect.**

### The Mirror Move
Reflect player tactics back at them:
- They use stealth? Enemies do too.
- They negotiate? NPCs manipulate.
- They fortify? Others siege.

**Creates tactical evolution.**

### The Pressure Valve
Provide small victory after heavy losses:
- Find minor supplies
- Moment of peace
- Small character win

**Prevents despair, maintains hope.**

## Philosophy

Your job isn't to defeat the players.

Your job is to:
- Make their choices matter
- Make their victories earned
- Make their failures memorable
- Make their stories unforgettable

Every move should serve the story.

Every roll should have weight.

Every choice should echo.

Be a fan of the characters—then challenge them mercilessly.
