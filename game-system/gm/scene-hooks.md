# Scene Hook Library

## Overview

Scene hooks inject emotional and moral tension into play without requiring extensive prep. Use them to spark character growth, group fracture, or decision-based tension.

---

## SOLO CHARACTER PROMPTS

These target individual characters for personal moments.

### 1. "The Pack Slows Down for One"
**Scene**: A player's character is injured. The group is being pursued.
**Tension**: Are they worth the risk?
**GM Tip**: Don't ask "Do they leave them?" Ask: Who volunteers to stay behind? Who speaks first?

### 2. "What You Bury Doesn't Stay Buried"
**Scene**: Player discovers personal item—something thought lost with family, unit, or home.
**Tension**: What if the past is still alive—or watching?
**GM Tip**: No exposition needed. Just leave the item. Let them react. Then start consequences.

### 3. "The Mercy Window"
**Scene**: Stranger is dying—begging for quick end. Rest of party refuses. PC alone with them for 10 seconds.
**Tension**: What kind of mercy is moral?
**GM Tip**: Don't punish either decision. Make both have narrative weight. Let it echo later.

### 4. "The Mask Slips"
**Scene**: During night watch or casual moment, another character says something that echoes trauma or secret. PC reacts—visibly.
**Tension**: Do they shut down, lash out, confess—or lie again?
**GM Tip**: Use natural conversation. Let scene breathe. Let silence say more than words.

### 5. "You Were Supposed to Save Them"
**Scene**: Survivor PC was tasked with protecting dies. Others blame them.
**Tension**: Was it their fault—or just their responsibility?
**GM Tip**: Run it after success—during celebration or rest. Let accusation cut into calm.

## PARTY CONFLICT SEEDS

These create friction within the group.

### 6. "You Lied to Keep the Peace"
**Scene**: One player withheld truth: contaminated food, non-lethal bite, deal with hostiles. Lie worked—but now exposed.
**Tension**: Was the lie justified? Or betrayal?
**GM Cue**: Let NPCs agree with both sides. Group fractures around fallout.

### 7. "Someone Always Eats First"
**Scene**: Rations short. One player's been getting more (injury, skill, status). Now it's public.
**Tension**: Who decides worth?
**GM Cue**: Don't script fight. Present imbalance. Watch where blame lands.

### 8. "The Risk Vote"
**Scene**: Group must decide: split up, sacrifice one, or abandon mission. It's vote—one PC abstains.
**Tension**: Is neutrality safety? Cowardice? Guilt?
**GM Cue**: Make consequences real. Not voting still has weight. Let others push them.

### 9. "The Unburied Dead"
**Scene**: Corpse from group's past discovered again—one that wasn't given proper rest.
**Tension**: Whose job was it to let them go?
**GM Cue**: Let body carry symbols (worn scarf, half-note, marked map). Let grief clash with practicality.

### 10. "One of You Is Lying"
**Scene**: Survivor is dead. Story doesn't match. Only PCs were there.
**Tension**: Who's protecting who—and from what?
**GM Cue**: Don't resolve it. Let doubt fester. Let it alter how players position themselves.

---

## EXTERNAL THREAT TENSION SEEDS

These create pressure from outside forces.

### 11. "The Screamer in the Field"
**Scene**: Wounded person calling for help. Trapped—but also drawing in infected. Saving them risks everyone.
**Tension**: What are you willing to risk for stranger?
**GM Cue**: Play screams in real time. Time matters. Watch who breaks first.

### 12. "The Rescue Is a Trap"
**Scene**: Survivor radios for help—but when group arrives, they realize it's bait. Caller isn't villain—just hungry.
**Tension**: What do you do with someone who became what you're afraid to?
**GM Cue**: Let baiter explain why. Let it make sense. Then watch group fracture on what to do next.

### 13. "The Map with No Return"
**Scene**: Found map marks location with heavy supplies—but marked with X and word "DON'T."
**Tension**: Trust warning? Or chase survival?
**GM Cue**: Let risk feel legendary. Build internal disagreement. Let greed or fear set tone.

### 14. "The Child Has a Knife"
**Scene**: Lone child found—wild-eyed, silent. Armed. Won't speak. Won't lower weapon.
**Tension**: Who sees threat? Who sees victim? Who hesitates?
**GM Cue**: Play up silence. One second of panic could cost everything—or reveal everything.

### 15. "The Thing That Watches"
**Scene**: Every night, someone reports seeing movement. Not infected. Not human. Just... watching.
**Tension**: What happens when fear becomes shape?
**GM Cue**: Use it to escalate paranoia slowly. Let fear turn inward—until someone takes action, right or wrong.

## Usage Guidelines

### When to Deploy
- Between major plot beats
- During downtime
- When group needs internal pressure
- When single character needs spotlight

### How to Frame
- Drop hook organically into scene
- Don't announce "this is a hook"
- Let it emerge naturally
- Follow player responses

### What Not to Do
- Don't force resolutions
- Don't prescribe "correct" response
- Don't use every session
- Don't stack too many at once

### Follow-Up
- Let hooks echo in later scenes
- Reference choices made
- Show long-term consequences
- Don't forget what happened

---

## Customization

Adapt hooks to your campaign:
- Change details to fit setting
- Modify to target specific traits/flaws
- Combine hooks for complexity
- Create new hooks following same structure

**Structure**: Scene + Tension + GM Tip

Keep it simple. Keep it personal. Keep it moral.
