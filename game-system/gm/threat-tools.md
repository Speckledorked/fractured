# Threat Tools

## Overview

Threat tools help GMs track escalating danger, manage pacing, and create tension through visual, trackable systems.

---

## Threat Clocks

### What Are Threat Clocks?

Visual trackers for escalating danger—segments that fill as situation worsens.

**Structure:**
- 4 segments: Fast-moving threats
- 6 segments: Slower-building danger
- 8 segments: Epic, campaign-spanning threats (rare)

**Visualization:**
```
4-Segment:  [_][_][_][_]
6-Segment:  [_][_][_][_][_][_]
Filling:    [X][X][_][_][_][_]
```

### When to Start a Clock

Create clock when:
- Danger is introduced but not immediate
- You need to track escalation
- Players need visible pressure
- Situation has buildup, not instant resolution

**Examples:**
- Infected trying to breach barricade
- Ally bleeding out
- Hostile patrol searching for players
- Structure collapsing
- Trust eroding between factions

### How to Advance Clocks

**Add segment when:**
- Players fail relevant roll
- Players take noisy/desperate action
- Time passes narratively (GM judgment)
- Players hesitate or stall
- Situation logically worsens

**Don't advance on:**
- Every failed roll (use judgment)
- Unrelated actions
- Arbitrary timing
- Just to punish players

### When Clock Fills

**Threat manifests completely:**
- Describe it cinematically
- Make it impactful
- Follow through fully
- Don't pull punches

**Examples:**
- Breach clock fills → Infected pour through
- Bleeding clock fills → Ally dies or becomes critical
- Search clock fills → Enemies find you
- Collapse clock fills → Building comes down

**After resolution:**
- Remove filled clock
- Create new threat or pressure
- Let aftermath create story
- Don't immediately replace with same threat

### Visible vs Hidden Clocks

**Visible Clocks (Show Players):**
- Builds transparent tension
- Players can react strategically
- Creates urgency openly
- Use when you want informed decisions

**Hidden Clocks (GM Only):**
- Creates uncertain danger
- Players can't metagame
- Surprise when clock fills
- Use when mystery serves story

**Mix both for variety.**

## Multiple Threat Clocks

### Layering Pressure

Run multiple clocks simultaneously to force prioritization:

**Example Scenario:**
- **Breach Clock** [X][X][_][_]: Infected breaking through door
- **Bleeding Clock** [X][X][X][_]: Ally dying from wound
- **Discovery Clock** [X][_][_][_]: Hostile patrol getting closer

**Player Dilemma:**
- Reinforce door? (Addresses Breach)
- Treat ally? (Addresses Bleeding)
- Hide evidence? (Addresses Discovery)
- Can't address all simultaneously

**This creates meaningful choices and tension.**

### Clock Interactions

Clocks can affect each other:
- Breach clock fills → Fighting infected advances Bleeding clock
- Loud combat → Advances Discovery clock
- Taking time for medical → Advances Breach clock

**Show interconnection to increase pressure.**

## Threat Clock Examples

### Combat Scenarios

**"The Breach"** (4 segments)
- Start: Infected hammering on barricaded door
- Fill: Door breaks, infected flood in
- Advances: Failed defense rolls, loud actions, time passing

**"Surrounded"** (6 segments)
- Start: Hostiles encircling position
- Fill: No escape route, full siege
- Advances: Scouting reports, time, failed stealth

**"Bleeding Out"** (4 segments)
- Start: Serious injury, needs treatment
- Fill: Death or permanent injury
- Advances: Time passing, rough movement, failed Medical

### Survival Scenarios

**"The Long Dark"** (6 segments)
- Start: Generator failing, running out of fuel
- Fill: Complete darkness, cold, vulnerability
- Advances: Time, fuel consumption, failed Engineering

**"Starvation"** (6 segments)
- Start: Food running critically low
- Fill: Collapse from hunger, -2 to all actions
- Advances: Days passing without food

**"Lost"** (6 segments)
- Start: Navigation gone wrong
- Fill: Hopelessly lost, supplies depleting
- Advances: Failed Survival rolls, wrong turns

### Social Scenarios

**"Trust Fracture"** (6 segments)
- Start: Lies or tension building
- Fill: Group splits, violence, or desertion
- Advances: Broken promises, arguments, betrayals

**"Negotiation Collapse"** (4 segments)
- Start: Diplomatic talks beginning to sour
- Fill: Negotiations fail, possible violence
- Advances: Insults, threats, failed Diplomacy

## Pressure Escalation Tools

### Escalation Ladder

Build pressure gradually:

**Level 1: Tension**
- Describe early warning signs
- Create atmosphere
- Plant seeds of danger
- No immediate threat

**Level 2: Urgency**
- Start threat clocks
- Make danger visible
- Add time pressure
- Force awareness

**Level 3: Crisis**
- Clocks advancing rapidly
- Multiple threats active
- Resources depleting
- Choices critical

**Level 4: Climax**
- Clocks near full
- All-or-nothing moments
- Everything at stake
- Resolution imminent

**Level 5: Aftermath**
- Deal with consequences
- Show cost of choices
- Reset or escalate further
- Breathing room

**Move through levels based on session pacing.**

### Pressure Valves

Provide relief to prevent player burnout:

**Small Victories:**
- Successfully complete objective
- Find needed supplies
- Moment of peace
- Relationship win

**Downtime Scenes:**
- Safe conversation
- Processing trauma
- Planning next move
- Character moments

**Humor:**
- Brief levity
- Gallows humor
- NPC comedy relief
- Natural joking

**Hope:**
- Glimpse of better future
- Good news from elsewhere
- Small kindness
- Reason to continue

**Balance tension and release.**

## Threat Categories

### Immediate Threats
**Timeframe**: Minutes to hours
**Examples**: Combat, chases, collapsing structures
**Clock Size**: 4 segments
**Pacing**: Fast, intense

### Building Threats
**Timeframe**: Hours to days
**Examples**: Resource depletion, disease progression, enemy pursuit
**Clock Size**: 6 segments
**Pacing**: Steady pressure

### Long-Term Threats
**Timeframe**: Weeks to seasons
**Examples**: Faction wars, environmental collapse, personal arcs
**Clock Size**: 8 segments or milestone-based
**Pacing**: Background pressure, occasional advances

### Combine for layered tension.**

## GM Threat Management

### Tracking Threats

**Simple Method:**
- Paper tracker with boxes
- Mark X when segment fills
- Note what each clock represents

**Visual Method:**
- Physical tokens or dice
- Move/add as clock advances
- Players can see some, not others

**Narrative Method:**
- Track mentally
- Describe progression verbally
- No visible tracker

**Choose method that fits your table.**

### Communicating Threats

**Describe progression:**
- "The door is splintering—two more impacts like that and it's down."
- "She's pale, shaking. You don't have much time."
- "You hear their radios crackling closer. They're narrowing the search."

**Show, don't just tell:**
- Use sensory details
- Make threats visceral
- Let players see evidence
- Build dread through description

### Adjusting Difficulty

**Too Easy:**
- Advance clocks faster
- Add more clocks
- Increase consequences
- Remove safety nets

**Too Hard:**
- Slow clock advancement
- Remove some clocks
- Provide resources
- Create opportunities

**Monitor table energy and adjust.**

## Threat Complications

### Compound Threats

Multiple threats that multiply each other:
- Infected breach + medical emergency
- Starvation + hostile pursuit
- Faction war + resource collapse

**Creates impossible choices.**

### Hidden Consequences

Threats that don't reveal until late:
- Infection spreading unseen
- Spy in group
- Slow poisoning
- Structural damage

**Creates paranoia and uncertainty.**

### Moral Threats

Dangers that test values:
- Must harm innocent to escape
- Sacrifice one to save many
- Betray values or die
- Become what you hate

**Creates Red Line moments.**

## Threat Resolution

### Success
- Clock eliminated
- Danger passes
- Victory earned
- Breathing room created

**Show cost even in success:**
- Resources spent
- Casualties taken
- Exhaustion
- Emotional toll

### Failure
- Clock fills completely
- Threat manifests fully
- Consequences real
- New situation emerges

**Make failure create story:**
- Force adaptation
- Create new pressure
- Reveal character
- Push arc forward

### Partial Success
- Clock partially filled but danger averted
- Threat postponed not eliminated
- Temporary solution
- Price paid for time

**Creates ongoing tension.**

## Integration with Other Systems

### Threats + Resources
- Clocks can deplete resources
- Resources can slow clocks
- Resource scarcity creates threats

### Threats + Crisis Engine
- Threats trigger Crisis Profiles
- Stress from threats accelerates trait evolution
- Threat resolution creates breakthrough moments

### Threats + Red Lines
- Filled clocks often create Red Line moments
- Threats force boundary tests
- Moral threats target Red Lines specifically

### Threats + Seasons
- Season 1: Fewer, simpler threats
- Season 2: Multiple, layered threats
- Season 3: Constant, severe threats
- Season 4: Existential, finale threats
