# Four Seasons Campaign Structure

## Overview

Fractured campaigns follow a **Four Seasons** structure—each season represents a phase of collapse and transformation. Characters who survive all four seasons will be fundamentally changed.

**Each Season**: 4-5 sessions
**Full Campaign**: 16-20 sessions total

---

## The Structure

### Season 1: The Break
*"You thought you'd have time. You were wrong."*

### Season 2: The Divide
*"Now it's just you... and whoever made it through."*

### Season 3: The Fall
*"The safe places are gone. So are the safe people."*

### Season 4: The Reckoning
*"You made it here. That doesn't mean you survived it."*

---

## SEASON 1: THE BREAK

### Overview
The world collapses. Order dissolves. Characters face their first impossible choices.

### Collapse Event
The outbreak explodes without warning:
- Shelters fail
- Authorities vanish
- Power, roads, and order dissolve overnight
- Everything familiar becomes hostile

### Narrative Pressure

**Early Sessions (1-2):**
- Choose what to carry—and who to leave
- First infected contact (close, personal, overwhelming)
- Refugees surge, supplies dwindle by the hour

**Later Sessions (3-5):**
- Establish temporary base or shelter
- Form group bonds under pressure
- Face first moral compromises
- Witness collapse of civilization

### Gameplay Focus

**Character Development:**
- Establish traits and flaws in action
- Form initial relationships
- Reveal backstories through crisis
- Plant seeds for arcs

**Mechanical Focus:**
- Learn core systems
- First combat encounters
- Resource scarcity introduction
- Basic survival challenges

### Red Line Moment
> **"You have five minutes. Who do you go back for?"**

First test of values. First loss. First guilt.

### Key Beats

**Beat 1**: Normal day interrupted by chaos
**Beat 2**: Immediate survival—flee or fight
**Beat 3**: Loss of something/someone important
**Beat 4**: First kill (human or infected)
**Beat 5**: Temporary safety established—but fragile

### Season 1 Outcomes

**Characters emerge:**
- Traumatized but functional
- With 1-2 Growth Mark advances
- Having faced first Red Line
- Bonded or fractured as group

**World state:**
- Known safe zones exist but strained
- Some authority remains
- Hope still exists
- Rules still matter (mostly)

---

## SEASON 2: THE DIVIDE

### Overview
Factions form. Resources dwindle. Internal conflict begins. Trust cracks.

### Collapse Event
Infection spreads faster than rumor:
- Group splits begin—some can't handle the weight
- Small factions form with different ethics
- Safe zones become contested territory
- Moral lines blur

### Narrative Pressure

**Early Sessions (6-8):**
- Decide: fortify, flee, or scavenge
- Rationing begins, hunger sets in
- First betrayals or abandonments
- Encounter other survivor groups

**Later Sessions (9-10):**
- Base becomes symbol of hope—or cage
- Internal power struggles
- Moral compromises deepen
- Choose who to help, who to turn away

### Gameplay Focus

**Character Development:**
- Trait evolution opportunities
- Flaw flare-ups under stress
- Relationship complications
- Crisis Profiles emerge

**Mechanical Focus:**
- Resource management crucial
- Base building/defense
- Factional interactions
- Harder moral choices

### Red Line Moment
> **"Someone has to go. Who decides—and why is it you?"**

Test of leadership. Test of worth. Test of mercy.

### Key Beats

**Beat 1**: Resources become critically scarce
**Beat 2**: Encounter hostile survivor faction
**Beat 3**: Internal group conflict erupts
**Beat 4**: Someone breaks under pressure
**Beat 5**: Choice that splits group or unifies it

### Scene Hooks for Season 2

Use from scene hook library:
- "Someone Always Eats First"
- "You Lied to Keep the Peace"
- "The Risk Vote"
- "The Unburied Dead"
- "One of You Is Lying"

### Season 2 Outcomes

**Characters emerge:**
- Hardened, cynical, or broken
- With 2-3 more advances
- Crisis Profiles active
- Relationships tested

**World state:**
- Few safe zones remain
- Authority is dead or tyrannical
- Hope is rationed
- Rules are negotiable

---

## SEASON 3: THE FALL

### Overview
Safe places vanish. Safe people die. Morality becomes luxury. Characters confront who they've become.

### Collapse Event
The world breaks completely:
- Herd sweeps through, wipes out known safe zones
- NPC betrayal, death, or madness
- Morale crashes
- Sanity rolls frequent

### Narrative Pressure

**Early Sessions (11-13):**
- Make brutal choices: mercy kills, deserter hunts, self-sacrifice
- Confront flaws—they're not quirks, they're threats
- Witness or commit atrocities
- Question if survival is worth it

**Later Sessions (14-15):**
- Base falls, transform, or abandon
- Personal losses mount
- Face Red Lines directly
- Final trait evolutions or flaw mutations

### Gameplay Focus

**Character Development:**
- Trait spiral or resilience paths chosen
- Flaw breakthrough or deepening
- Red Line proximity constant
- Arcs reach crisis points

**Mechanical Focus:**
- Resource collapse
- Constant threat
- Desperate missions
- High mortality risk

### Red Line Moment
> **"What part of yourself do you kill so the rest can live?"**

Test of identity. Test of limits. Test of humanity.

### Key Beats

**Beat 1**: Everything secure is destroyed
**Beat 2**: Must make impossible choice
**Beat 3**: Character breaks or transforms
**Beat 4**: Point of no return crossed
**Beat 5**: New reality accepted or rejected

### Scene Hooks for Season 3

Use from scene hook library:
- "The Mercy Window"
- "The Mask Slips"
- "You Were Supposed to Save Them"
- "The Rescue Is a Trap"
- "The Child Has a Knife"

### Season 3 Outcomes

**Characters emerge:**
- Transformed, broken, or dead
- With 2-3 more advances
- Having crossed or held Red Lines
- Fundamentally changed

**World state:**
- No safe zones
- No authority
- No hope (or secret hope)
- No rules

---

## SEASON 4: THE RECKONING

### Overview
Final decisions. Final transformations. Final costs. Characters define legacies.

### Collapse Event
World-shifting decision arrives:
- Escape attempt
- Final stand
- Collapse into unknown
- All arcs resolve

### Narrative Pressure

**Early Sessions (16-18):**
- Choose who you are now
- Face Red Line directly
- End something permanent
- Reckon with all you've done

**Final Sessions (19-20):**
- Last resource run
- Last betrayal
- Last mercy
- Last choice

### Gameplay Focus

**Character Development:**
- Arc resolution
- Final trait evolution
- Flaw breakthrough or spiral
- Legacy established

**Mechanical Focus:**
- Final mission
- Ultimate stakes
- No safety nets
- Everything on line

### Red Line Moment
> **"To live... what would you let yourself become?"**

Final test. Final choice. Final transformation.

### Key Beats

**Beat 1**: Final mission/goal emerges
**Beat 2**: Gather resources/allies for final push
**Beat 3**: Point of no return
**Beat 4**: Climax—everything decided
**Beat 5**: Epilogue—show what remains

### Resolution Types

**Escape**: Leave the wasteland, uncertain future
**Stand**: Defend something worth dying for
**Rebuild**: Start creating new world
**Fall**: Everyone dies or worse
**Transform**: Become something else entirely

### Season 4 Outcomes

**Characters emerge:**
- Victorious or defeated
- Alive or dead
- Transformed forever
- With complete arcs

**World state:**
- Depends on choices
- New beginning or final end
- Hope rekindled or extinguished
- Legacy established

## Campaign Pacing

### Session-by-Session Arc

**Season 1 (Sessions 1-5):**
- Session 1: The outbreak begins
- Session 2: First night survival
- Session 3: Establishing base
- Session 4: First moral crisis
- Session 5: Season climax—first Red Line

**Season 2 (Sessions 6-10):**
- Session 6: Resource scarcity hits
- Session 7: Faction encounter
- Session 8: Internal conflict
- Session 9: Betrayal or sacrifice
- Session 10: Season climax—group defining choice

**Season 3 (Sessions 11-15):**
- Session 11: Safe zone falls
- Session 12: Desperate measures
- Session 13: Point of no return
- Session 14: Red Line proximity
- Session 15: Season climax—transformation or break

**Season 4 (Sessions 16-20):**
- Session 16: Final mission emerges
- Session 17: Preparation and goodbyes
- Session 18: First phase of finale
- Session 19: Climax—everything decided
- Session 20: Epilogue—what remains

### Flexible Pacing

Seasons can be:
- **Shorter**: 3 sessions (faster campaign)
- **Longer**: 6 sessions (deeper development)
- **Variable**: Season 1-2 longer, 3-4 shorter

**Follow the fiction, not the formula.**

## GM Tools

### Season Prep Checklist

**Before Each Season:**
- [ ] Review character arcs—which advance this season?
- [ ] Prepare season-specific scenes
- [ ] Identify potential Red Line moments
- [ ] Plan resource pressure escalation
- [ ] Consider NPC fates
- [ ] Prepare collapse event

### Season Transition

**Between Seasons:**
- Debrief previous season
- Allow character reflection
- Show passage of time
- Update world state
- Introduce new threats
- Plant seeds for next season

### Season Climax Design

Each season ends with:
1. **Impossible choice**
2. **High stakes**
3. **Permanent consequences**
4. **Character revelation**
5. **World transformation**

**Make it hurt. Make it matter.**

## Integration

### Seasons + Arcs
- Primary arcs span full campaign
- Secondary arcs resolve per season
- Each season tests different arc aspects

### Seasons + Growth Marks
- Season 1: 1-2 advances expected
- Season 2: 2-3 advances expected
- Season 3: 2-3 advances expected
- Season 4: 1-2 advances expected
- **Total**: 6-10 advances per campaign

### Seasons + Red Lines
- Season 1: First Red Line proximity
- Season 2: Red Line tested
- Season 3: Red Line crossed or held
- Season 4: Red Line defines finale

### Seasons + Resources
- Season 1: Scarcity begins
- Season 2: Rationing critical
- Season 3: Desperation sets in
- Season 4: Nothing left but will

---

## Philosophy

The Four Seasons aren't just campaign structure—they're character transformation engine.

**Season 1** asks: *Can you survive?*
**Season 2** asks: *Can you stay human?*
**Season 3** asks: *Can you live with who you've become?*
**Season 4** asks: *Was it worth it?*

Characters who reach the end aren't the same people who started.

That's not a bug.

That's the entire point.
