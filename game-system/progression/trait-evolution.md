# Trait Evolution

## Overview

Traits evolve through testing and pressure. Characters don't stay the same—their strengths refine into greatness or decay into obsession. Every trait has a spiral path and a resilience path.

## Evolution Mechanics

### When Traits Evolve

Traits are ready to evolve when:
- **Used successfully 3+ times** in high-stakes situations
- **Tested during Crisis Frame** with related Crisis Profile active
- **Character survives Red Line moment** related to trait
- **GM determines** enough time/pressure has passed

### Evolution Trigger

Player must spend **1 Character Advance** (3 Growth Marks) and choose:
- **Spiral Path**: Trait becomes obsessive, rigid, or harmful
- **Resilience Path**: Trait becomes refined, powerful, or transcendent

### Evolution Is Permanent

Once evolved, the old trait is replaced. Can't revert (though further evolution is possible).

---

## Evolution Paths

Every trait has two potential directions:

### Spiral Path (Decay)
**When it happens:**
- High stress, unresolved trauma
- Crisis Profile deepening
- Repeated failures related to trait
- Character giving in to darker impulses

**Results:**
- Trait becomes more extreme
- Mechanical benefit may increase but at cost
- Narrative complications multiply
- May eventually become a flaw

**Example:**
"Protector" → "Blind Guardian"
- Still protects, but without judgment
- +2 when defending others, -2 to threat assessment
- Will protect even unworthy or dangerous individuals
- Creates group conflict

### Resilience Path (Refinement)
**When it happens:**
- Successfully navigating pressure
- Processing trauma healthily
- Support from group
- Red Line held or crossed with growth

**Results:**
- Trait becomes stronger and wiser
- Mechanical benefit increases cleanly
- Narrative opportunities expand
- May inspire others

**Example:**
"Protector" → "Shield of Many"
- Protects wisely and effectively
- +2 when defending others
- Can protect multiple targets simultaneously
- Inspires defensive positioning in allies

## Common Evolution Examples

### Psychological Traits

**Unshakable**
- Spiral: "Emotional Bottleneck" (can't process feelings, eventual catastrophic break)
- Resilience: "Stoic Guardian" (unbreakable center for others, processes privately)

**Hyperfocused**
- Spiral: "Tunnel Vision" (misses everything outside focus, dangerous blindness)
- Resilience: "Master Technician" (precision under pressure without losing awareness)

**Calculating**
- Spiral: "Emotionless Planner" (loses humanity for efficiency, isolated)
- Resilience: "Strategic Mastermind" (thinks three steps ahead, inspires confidence)

**Curious**
- Spiral: "Unleashed Catalyst" (curiosity creates disasters, can't stop investigating)
- Resilience: "Field Scholar" (knowledge becomes power, safe investigation)

### Social Traits

**Charismatic**
- Spiral: "Manipulator" (uses charm to control, loses genuine connections)
- Resilience: "Rallying Flame" (genuinely inspiring, brings out best in others)

**Trustworthy**
- Spiral: "Fall from Grace" (reputation shattered by one mistake, never recovers)
- Resilience: "Moral Compass" (anchor for others, unimpeachable integrity)

**Diplomatic**
- Spiral: "Peace at Any Price" (enables harmful compromises, loses spine)
- Resilience: "The Negotiator" (resolves impossible conflicts, respected mediator)

**Empathic Speaker**
- Spiral: "Compassion Fatigue" (absorbs too much pain, collapses)
- Resilience: "Emotional Linchpin" (holds group together, manages emotions)

### Tactical Traits

**Instinctive**
- Spiral: "Loose Cannon" (acts without thinking, dangerous unpredictability)
- Resilience: "Combat Natural" (perfect instincts, reads situations instantly)

**Planner**
- Spiral: "Gridlocked Analyst" (paralyzed by planning, can't act)
- Resilience: "Architect" (builds perfect strategies, adapts fluidly)

**Frontliner**
- Spiral: "Cratered Body" (too much damage taken, permanent injuries)
- Resilience: "War Wall" (unstoppable vanguard, intimidating presence)

**Watchdog**
- Spiral: "Paranoid Seer" (sees threats everywhere, burns out)
- Resilience: "Early Warning System" (saves group repeatedly, trusted eyes)

### Resource Traits

**Efficient Forager**
- Spiral: "Poison Picker" (finds wrong things, overconfident in dangerous ruins)
- Resilience: "Salvage King/Queen" (legendary scavenger, always finds what's needed)

**Builder's Mind**
- Spiral: "Sunk Cost Trapper" (trapped by own constructions, won't adapt)
- Resilience: "Compound Architect" (builds lasting safety, masterful engineer)

**Medic**
- Spiral: "PTSD Healer" (haunted by those lost, breaks under pressure)
- Resilience: "Lifeline" (saves everyone, calm under worst conditions)

### Moral Traits

**Code-Follower**
- Spiral: "Broken Idealist" (code shatters, loses all moral direction)
- Resilience: "Saint" (uncompromising integrity, beacon of values)

**Second-Chancer**
- Spiral: "Dead Believer" (trust kills them, naive to the end)
- Resilience: "Hope-Bearer" (redeems the broken, changes hearts)

**Guardian**
- Spiral: "Blind Guardian" (protects unwisely, enables harm)
- Resilience: "Shield of the Few" (protection with wisdom, selective care)

## Evolution Process

### Step 1: Recognize Readiness
GM identifies trait ready for evolution:
- Trait used repeatedly in critical moments
- Character arc aligns with evolution
- Dramatic moment makes evolution appropriate
- Player has advance available

### Step 2: Present Options
GM presents both paths to player:

> "Your 'Protector' trait has been tested repeatedly. You've thrown yourself into danger again and again. It's changing you. When you're ready to advance, you can evolve it:
>
> **Spiral: 'Blind Guardian'**
> You protect without judgment, even the unworthy. +2 when defending, but -2 to threat assessment. You can't stop yourself.
>
> **Resilience: 'Shield of Many'**
> You protect with wisdom and sacrifice. +2 when defending, and you can protect multiple targets. Your presence inspires others' defensive actions."

### Step 3: Player Chooses
Player considers:
- Character arc direction
- Recent events
- Group needs
- Personal preference

**No wrong choice**—both are valid character development.

### Step 4: Apply Evolution
- Remove old trait from sheet
- Add new trait
- Note evolution in character history
- Spend 1 Character Advance
- Roleplay the shift

### Step 5: Reflect Change
Evolution should be visible:
- Other characters notice
- Player describes how it manifests
- GM creates opportunities to use evolved trait
- Narrative acknowledges transformation

## Multiple Evolutions

Some traits can evolve multiple times:

**Example: "Protector" Chain**

**Stage 1**: Protector
- Base trait, +1 when defending

**Stage 2a (Spiral)**: Blind Guardian
- +2 defending, -2 threat assessment

**Stage 3a (Further Spiral)**: Martyr's End
- Will die to protect anyone, even enemies
- Group morale penalty when present (they fear for you)

**Stage 2b (Resilience)**: Shield of Many
- +2 defending, can protect multiple

**Stage 3b (Further Resilience)**: Guardian Angel
- +2 defending, automatically negate one fatal blow per session
- Inspire defensive positioning in all allies

### Evolution Limits
- Maximum 3 evolutions per trait per campaign
- Each evolution requires separate advance
- Must justify continued evolution through play
- GM may cap evolutions for balance

---

## Forced Evolution

Sometimes traits evolve without player choice:

### Automatic Spiral
Trait spirals automatically when:
- Character reaches critical stress
- Red Line crossed in related way
- Crisis Profile fully activated
- Character has ignored warnings

**Example:**
"Protector" automatically becomes "Blind Guardian" after character takes damage for unworthy person three times, ignoring all warnings.

### Involuntary Transformation
Rare, but possible:
- Traumatic event fundamentally changes character
- Trait can no longer function as before
- Character's worldview shifts dramatically

**GM should telegraph this clearly and give player agency when possible.**

## Evolution and Story

### Evolution Moments
Make trait evolution visible:

**Before Evolution:**
> "You've always protected others. But lately, you hesitate. You weigh the cost. You're changing."

**At Evolution:**
> "As you step between them and the infected, something shifts. You're not just protecting anymore—you're calculating. Who needs it most? Who deserves it? You've become the Shield of Many, and it feels right."

**After Evolution:**
> "The group looks to you differently now. When danger comes, they position themselves around you instinctively. You've become their center."

### Evolution Triggers
Create scenes that prompt evolution:
- Test trait to breaking point
- Force choice between spiral and resilience
- Offer moment of clarity or descent
- Make evolution feel earned, not arbitrary

### Group Reaction
Other characters notice evolution:
- NPCs comment on change
- PCs respond to new behavior
- Relationships shift
- Reputation changes

---

## GM Guidelines

### Pacing Evolution
- 1st evolution: After 4-6 sessions
- 2nd evolution: After 8-12 sessions
- 3rd evolution: After 12-16 sessions

### Balancing Evolutions
- Not all traits need to evolve
- Some characters evolve one trait deeply
- Others evolve multiple traits once each
- Follow character arcs, not formulas

### Supporting Player Choice
- Present both options fairly
- Don't punish spiral choices
- Don't reward only resilience
- Let darkness be compelling
- Make both paths interesting

### Evolution and Flaws
- Spiral traits may become flaws
- Resilience traits may resolve flaws
- Flaws can evolve into traits (via Breakthrough)
- Evolution connects to broader character arc

---

## Philosophy

Traits evolve because people change under pressure.

No one emerges from trauma unchanged. No strength remains static when tested. Every trait is an invitation to become something different—better or worse, refined or broken, evolved or devolved.

The question isn't whether your traits will change.

The question is: when they do, who will you become?
