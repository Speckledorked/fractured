# Arc System

## Overview

Personal arcs are ongoing character storylines that evolve through play. They're shaped by traits, flaws, relationships, and the choices characters make under pressure.

## Arc Structure

### What Is a Personal Arc?

A personal arc is:
- **Character-driven narrative thread** running through campaign
- **Rooted in backstory, traits, or flaws**
- **Resolved through choices and growth**
- **Tracked by GM but shaped by player**

### Arc Components

Every arc has:
1. **Catalyst**: Inciting event or unresolved past
2. **Question**: What does character need to resolve?
3. **Escalation**: How pressure builds
4. **Climax**: Moment of choice or transformation
5. **Resolution**: How it changes the character

---

## Arc Types

### Redemption Arc
**Catalyst**: Past mistake or failure
**Question**: Can they forgive themselves or earn forgiveness?
**Escalation**: Reminders of failure, similar situations
**Climax**: Chance to succeed where they failed before—or repeat mistake
**Resolution**: Forgiveness, acceptance, or continued guilt

**Example:**
Former soldier who abandoned unit now protects every civilian at personal cost.

### Revenge Arc
**Catalyst**: Loss of loved one or betrayal
**Question**: Will vengeance heal them or hollow them?
**Escalation**: Clues to enemy, near misses, moral compromises
**Climax**: Face the one responsible
**Resolution**: Kill and feel empty, spare and find peace, or die trying

**Example:**
Hunter seeking infected who killed their family, slowly losing humanity.

### Identity Arc
**Catalyst**: Question of who they are post-collapse
**Question**: Who am I now? Who do I want to be?
**Escalation**: Choices that define them, others' expectations
**Climax**: Moment where they must claim identity
**Resolution**: New self emerges, clear and defined

**Example:**
Former teacher becoming killer, struggling with who they've become.

### Loyalty Arc
**Catalyst**: Torn between two loyalties (person, group, ideal)
**Question**: Who/what do I choose?
**Escalation**: Competing demands, irreconcilable needs
**Climax**: Forced to choose one over other
**Resolution**: Choice made, relationship changed forever

**Example:**
Character torn between survival group and search for missing sibling.

### Belief Arc
**Catalyst**: Core belief challenged by reality
**Question**: Was I wrong? Can I change?
**Escalation**: Evidence mounting against belief
**Climax**: Undeniable proof forces reckoning
**Resolution**: Belief shatters, evolves, or doubles down

**Example:**
Idealist believing people are good faces brutal truth of human nature.

### Discovery Arc
**Catalyst**: Mystery from past or about world
**Question**: What really happened? What's the truth?
**Escalation**: Clues found, answers lead to more questions
**Climax**: Truth revealed
**Resolution**: Truth brings closure, pain, or new questions

**Example:**
Character searching for truth about outbreak origin, discovering conspiracy.

### Sacrifice Arc
**Catalyst**: Realization someone must pay cost
**Question**: Am I willing to be the one?
**Escalation**: Cost becoming clearer, alternatives failing
**Climax**: Moment to sacrifice or let others pay
**Resolution**: Sacrifice made or avoided, guilt or peace follows

**Example:**
Leader realizing group's survival requires their death or exile.

### Transformation Arc
**Catalyst**: Breaking point or revelation
**Question**: Can I become someone different?
**Escalation**: Old self failing, new self emerging
**Climax**: Symbolic death of old identity
**Resolution**: Rebirth as new person, for better or worse

**Example:**
Coward becoming hero through repeated acts of courage under pressure.

## Arc Development

### Creating Arcs

**During Character Creation:**
- Emerge from quiz responses
- Connect to backstory elements
- Link to traits/flaws
- Tie to Red Lines

**Example:**
Quiz shows guilt over abandoning family → Redemption Arc
Trait "Protector" + Flaw "Guilt Magnet" → Must save everyone to prove worth

### Arc Beats

Track arcs through beats:

**Beat 1: Introduction** (Sessions 1-2)
- Hint at arc through roleplay
- Plant seeds in backstory
- Show character's current state

**Beat 2: Complication** (Sessions 3-5)
- Introduce element that challenges character
- Test their current approach
- Escalate stakes

**Beat 3: Escalation** (Sessions 6-8)
- Multiple challenges related to arc
- Character's methods failing or tested
- Relationships strained by arc

**Beat 4: Crisis** (Sessions 9-12)
- Arc reaches breaking point
- Must confront core question
- Red Line proximity

**Beat 5: Resolution** (Sessions 13-16)
- Arc concludes one way or another
- Character transformed
- New status quo established

### Multiple Arcs

Characters can have:
- **1 primary arc** (runs full campaign)
- **1-2 secondary arcs** (shorter, 4-8 sessions)
- **Episodic moments** (single-session threads)

**Don't overload characters**—focus matters.

---

## Arc Pacing

### Season Integration

**Season 1: The Break**
- Introduce primary arc
- Plant seeds for future
- Establish character baseline

**Season 2: The Divide**
- Escalate primary arc
- Introduce secondary arcs
- Test character's approach

**Season 3: The Fall**
- Primary arc reaches crisis
- Force arc-defining choices
- Push toward resolution or transformation

**Season 4: The Reckoning**
- Resolve primary arc
- Close secondary arcs
- Show permanent changes

### Session-by-Session

Not every session advances every arc:
- Some sessions focus on one character's arc
- Others advance multiple arcs lightly
- Downtime sessions develop arcs through roleplay
- Crisis sessions test arcs through action

**Rotate focus**—give each character spotlight.

### Arc Prompts

Use these to advance arcs:

**Redemption Arc:**
- Encounter similar situation to past failure
- Meet someone who knows their past
- Must choose between safety and redemption
- Discover past wasn't entirely their fault

**Revenge Arc:**
- Find clue leading to target
- Encounter proxy or ally of target
- Must compromise morals to get closer
- Discover truth about what happened

**Identity Arc:**
- Forced to use old skills they've rejected
- Someone names them by old title/role
- Must choose between old self and new
- Mirror moment with NPC showing who they're becoming

**Loyalty Arc:**
- Both loyalties demand action simultaneously
- Must reveal loyalty publicly
- One loyalty tested by crisis
- Discovery that loyalties are irreconcilable

**Belief Arc:**
- Evidence contradicting belief
- NPC embodies belief's failure
- Must act against belief to survive
- Revelation that belief was based on lie

**Discovery Arc:**
- Major clue found
- NPC offers information for price
- Location holds answers—and danger
- Truth revealed partially

**Sacrifice Arc:**
- Cost becomes personal
- Alternative solutions fail
- Someone offers to take their place
- Moment arrives where sacrifice is needed

**Transformation Arc:**
- Old identity fails catastrophically
- New identity demanded by situation
- Symbolic breaking of old life
- Others recognize change in them

## Arc Resolution

### Types of Resolution

**Success**: Character achieves arc goal
- Redemption earned
- Revenge taken
- Identity claimed
- Loyalty chosen
- Belief confirmed
- Truth discovered
- Sacrifice made
- Transformation complete

**Failure**: Character fails to achieve goal
- Redemption denied
- Revenge missed or hollow
- Identity lost
- Loyalty torn apart
- Belief shattered
- Truth hidden
- Sacrifice refused
- Transformation incomplete

**Twist**: Arc resolves unexpectedly
- Redemption unnecessary (wasn't their fault)
- Revenge pointless (target already dead)
- Identity irrelevant (world changed)
- Loyalty betrayed both ways
- Belief partially true
- Truth worse than imagined
- Sacrifice rejected by recipient
- Transformation involuntary

**Ongoing**: Arc doesn't resolve, transforms
- New arc emerges from old
- Question shifts
- Character continues journey
- Some arcs have no clean endings

### Resolution Rewards

When arc resolves, character may:
- Gain bonus Growth Mark
- Earn specific trait related to arc
- Resolve related flaw
- Unlock new narrative options
- Achieve peace or closure

**Or:**
- Gain new flaw from failed arc
- Spiral existing trait
- Create new arc from consequences
- Face permanent change

## Integration with Other Systems

### Arcs + Traits/Flaws
- Arcs emerge from traits and flaws
- Arc resolution can evolve traits
- Arc completion can breakthrough flaws
- Traits/flaws complicate arc progress

### Arcs + Red Lines
- Arcs often lead to Red Line moments
- Red Lines define arc climaxes
- Crossing Red Line resolves some arcs
- Holding Red Line transforms others

### Arcs + Growth Marks
- Arc advancement earns Growth Marks
- Arc climax moments count for reflection questions
- Arc resolution may grant bonus marks
- Arcs create opportunities for growth

### Arcs + Seasons
- Arcs structure campaign narrative
- Seasons provide natural arc checkpoints
- Some arcs resolve per season
- Primary arc spans full campaign

## Example Arc

**Character**: Marcus, former firefighter
**Primary Arc**: Redemption
**Question**: Can he forgive himself for failing to save his crew?

**Beat 1 (Session 1-2)**: Introduction
- Marcus is overprotective, won't let others risk themselves
- Flashback reveals he survived while crew died
- He carries dead captain's badge

**Beat 2 (Session 3-5)**: Complication
- Group enters burning building, Marcus freezes
- Another character nearly dies because Marcus hesitated
- Marcus becomes more reckless to compensate

**Beat 3 (Session 6-8)**: Escalation
- Discovers one of his crew is alive—as infected
- Must choose: mercy kill or walk away
- Kills them, retraumatizing himself
- Trait "Protector" at risk of spiraling

**Beat 4 (Session 9-11)**: Crisis
- Building collapses, teammate trapped in fire
- Exact scenario that killed his crew
- Must enter alone or let teammate die
- Red Line moment: "I won't let others die for me"

**Climax Choice:**
- **Enters**: Saves teammate, proves he's redeemed himself
- **Doesn't Enter**: Teammate dies, arc spirals to self-destruction
- **Forces Someone Else**: Violates Red Line, transforms arc into guilt spiral

**Beat 5 (Session 12)**: Resolution
- If saved teammate: Forgives himself, evolves to "Guardian Angel"
- If failed: Gains "Death Wish" flaw, arc continues darker
- If violated Red Line: Transformation arc begins—who is he now?

---

## Philosophy

Arcs are the story you tell about becoming someone different.

They're not quests to complete or achievements to unlock. They're questions you carry until pressure forces an answer.

Every arc asks: **Who are you when everything you believed is tested?**

And every resolution shows: **This is who you became.**
