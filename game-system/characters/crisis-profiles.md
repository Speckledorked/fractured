# Crisis Profiles

## Overview

Crisis Profiles are behavioral archetypes that emerge when characters are pushed to their limits. They define who you become when everything falls apart—not who you are when things are stable.

## Purpose

Crisis Profiles:
- Reveal character under extreme pressure
- Guide GM characterization during stress
- Create predictable-yet-complex behavior patterns
- Enable character evolution arcs
- Provide framework for group dynamics

## Structure

Each profile includes:
- **Core Instinct**: Fundamental drive under pressure
- **Crisis Trigger**: What activates this profile
- **Primary Response**: First behavioral reaction
- **Secondary Response**: What happens if pressure continues
- **Behavioral Signs**: GM cues for portraying the profile
- **Spiral Path**: Where this goes if things worsen
- **Resilience Path**: Where this goes if navigated well
- **Group Effect**: Impact on team dynamics

## Activation

Profiles emerge when:
- Character reaches moderate-high stress
- Specific triggers align with profile description
- GM determines it fits character arc
- Multiple crisis situations accumulate

**Profiles are GM-only tools**—don't tell players their profile unless they ask.

## THE 15 CRISIS PROFILES

### 01: THE FRACTURER
**Core Instinct**: Emotional Chaos

**Crisis Trigger**: Grief, failure, or betrayal from within the group

**Primary Response**: Outbursts, blame-shifting, spirals of panic or violence

**Secondary Response**: Emotional volatility, sabotage, impulsive risk-taking

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Shaky hands, pacing, emotional flooding
- Aggressively interrupts or undermines others mid-crisis
- Hyperfocuses on blame ("This is your fault")
- Starts fights or breaks supplies impulsively

**Spiral Path**: Becomes destabilizing liability. May provoke mutiny, desertion, or internal violence.

**Resilience Path**: If supported or emotionally grounded, may transform into fierce truth-teller or morale flint.

**Group Effect**:
- Morale drops fast if unchecked
- Other players may splinter into factions or freeze
- NPCs lose confidence or mimic emotional chaos

**GM Use**: Deploy when group needs internal conflict or when player character has unprocessed trauma.

---

### 02: THE MASK
**Core Instinct**: Emotional Concealment

**Crisis Trigger**: Internal fear, vulnerability, or pressure to lead without showing cracks

**Primary Response**: Silence, control of outward emotion, hidden collapse

**Secondary Response**: Subtle withdrawal, suppressed grief or panic

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Flat voice, blank expressions, polished answers
- Avoids personal questions or reveals
- Takes on more responsibility than needed
- Dismisses empathy or soft talk

**Spiral Path**: May suddenly implode or make catastrophic choices in isolation. Risk of disappearing or suicide.

**Resilience Path**: Can become quiet center others depend on—emotionally invisible but unbreakable.

**Group Effect**:
- Others may feel comfort—or alienated
- Emotional friction builds without clear source
- NPCs may become suspicious or over-trusting

**GM Use**: Perfect for stoic characters or those hiding vulnerability. Creates tension through absence of reaction.

---

### 03: THE FIXER
**Core Instinct**: Control Through Action

**Crisis Trigger**: Perceived loss of group cohesion, helplessness, or failure to save someone

**Primary Response**: Hyper-tasking, micromanaging others, overextension

**Secondary Response**: Burnout, quiet resentment, collapse after success/failure

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Sleep deprivation, hoarding of tools/tasks
- Orders others around without consensus
- Neglects emotional needs for productivity
- May "fix" others instead of situations

**Spiral Path**: Becomes controlling force, disregarding human cost. Cracks if deprived of action.

**Resilience Path**: Refocuses efforts toward shared systems. Builds lasting infrastructure and earned trust.

**Group Effect**:
- Helps at first, but may trigger resistance
- Lowers morale if others feel incompetent
- Raises tension in downtime or discussion scenes

**GM Use**: Great for characters with high Engineering or Medical. Creates tension through excessive helpfulness.

---

### 04: THE GHOST
**Core Instinct**: Withdrawal

**Crisis Trigger**: Interpersonal conflict, traumatic reminders, perceived rejection or failure

**Primary Response**: Emotional and/or physical isolation

**Secondary Response**: Non-participation, disappearance, stealth-driven escapism

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Stops speaking unless spoken to
- Sleeps in different location or doesn't sleep
- Misses meetings, fades from group dynamics
- Only surfaces in tactical/logistical moments

**Spiral Path**: Becomes unreachable—may walk off alone, fail to warn others, or break silently.

**Resilience Path**: Can evolve into vigilant shadow, offering protection from fringes.

**Group Effect**:
- Feels like emotional abandonment
- Others may lash out or overcompensate
- NPCs assume instability or mental breakdown

**GM Use**: For withdrawn or traumatized characters. Creates tension through absence.

---

### 05: THE MARTYR
**Core Instinct**: Self-Sacrifice

**Crisis Trigger**: Belief they've failed, survivor's guilt, or need to redeem a loss

**Primary Response**: Voluntarily takes every risk, prioritizes others' survival

**Secondary Response**: Willingly absorbs damage or consequences for others

**Behavioral Signs**:
- "Send me." "I'll hold them."
- Gives up rations, gear, or medical care
- Refuses sleep or safety until others are secure
- Delivers final-sounding goodbyes frequently

**Spiral Path**: Dies unnecessarily, breaks group morale, leaves others guilty or angry.

**Resilience Path**: Inspires others to carry hope and responsibility. Becomes spiritual leader or symbol.

**Group Effect**:
- Emotionally potent—but often tragic
- Others may start copying them or trying to stop them
- NPCs idolize or exploit the behavior

**GM Use**: For self-sacrificing or guilt-driven characters. Creates tension through dangerous heroism.

---

### 06: THE ANCHOR
**Core Instinct**: Stabilization

**Crisis Trigger**: Group panic, leadership absence, cascading trauma

**Primary Response**: Emotional grounding of others, quiet action

**Secondary Response**: Internalized stress, denial of personal needs

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Speaks calmly, focuses group
- Uses body language to hold space (hand on shoulder, eye contact)
- Deprioritizes own safety or grief
- Becomes "last standing" when others falter

**Spiral Path**: Burns out slowly. May suffer breakdown when alone or post-crisis.

**Resilience Path**: Evolves into spiritual or emotional leader—respected, even if quiet.

**Group Effect**:
- Provides cohesion
- Boosts morale under pressure
- Their collapse can be catastrophic

**GM Use**: For high Diplomacy or Sanity characters. Their stability holds group together—until it doesn't.

---

### 07: THE FIREBRAND
**Core Instinct**: Confrontation

**Crisis Trigger**: Injustice, leadership breakdown, moral ambiguity

**Primary Response**: Verbal or physical confrontation

**Secondary Response**: Splinter factioning, calls for action or rebellion

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Demands answers, decisions, or justice
- Uses sharp language or stirs others emotionally
- Pushes against leadership or caution
- Can inspire or inflame depending on context

**Spiral Path**: Leads mutiny, fractures team, causes reactive violence.

**Resilience Path**: Catalyst for courageous decisions. Can drive moral clarity if channeled.

**Group Effect**:
- Polarizing force
- Can unite or divide rapidly
- NPCs may fear or follow them

**GM Use**: For passionate or justice-driven characters. Creates immediate conflict.

---

### 08: THE CHAMELEON
**Core Instinct**: Adaptive Masking

**Crisis Trigger**: Emotional threat, lack of identity, group friction

**Primary Response**: Becomes what others need—at cost of authenticity

**Secondary Response**: Identity confusion, secret resentment

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Shifts tone, role, or values based on who they're with
- Echoes others' speech or decisions
- Avoids conflict but holds internal tension
- Confesses uncertainty or guilt when isolated

**Spiral Path**: Loses sense of self. May self-destruct or betray group to feel needed.

**Resilience Path**: Learns self-worth, becomes adaptive without erasure.

**Group Effect**:
- Often underestimated
- Can fill gaps—but destabilizes when questioned
- Encourages others to reflect on their own behavior

**GM Use**: For socially flexible or uncertain characters. Creates tension through shapeshifting identity.

---

### 09: THE COMMANDER
**Core Instinct**: Authoritarian Control

**Crisis Trigger**: Chaos, weak leadership, moral ambiguity

**Primary Response**: Takes charge—hard. Demands obedience.

**Secondary Response**: Overruling others, unilateral decisions, forced order

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Shifts into formal tone
- Uses command language ("You will," "Stand down")
- Cuts off debate
- Takes high-risk or ethically gray actions for "good of group"

**Spiral Path**: Becomes tyrant. Loses group support, enforces control through threat.

**Resilience Path**: Forges hardened, respected leadership through earned results.

**Group Effect**:
- Short-term clarity or chaos depending on player buy-in
- NPCs align quickly—unless already distrustful
- May save or doom group based on decisions

**GM Use**: For high Combat or low Diplomacy characters thrust into leadership. Creates power struggle tension.

---

### 10: THE PROPHET
**Core Instinct**: Symbolic Meaning

**Crisis Trigger**: Cataclysmic loss, moral collapse, spiritual trauma

**Primary Response**: Frames events in prophecy, ritual, or "messages"

**Secondary Response**: Claims vision, creates strange guidance or cult behavior

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Begins speaking in metaphors, symbolism, or strange logic
- Claims dreams, visions, or unseen forces are speaking
- Assigns meaning to group deaths or failures
- Becomes fixated on omens or fateful signs

**Spiral Path**: Draws others into delusion. May lead people into ruin for symbolic ends.

**Resilience Path**: Offers unshakable belief and meaning—keeps others grounded when nothing else makes sense.

**Group Effect**:
- Inspires awe, distrust, or obsession
- May polarize spiritual vs rational survivors
- Useful in cultic, mystic, or collapse-focused arcs

**GM Use**: For characters with "Spirit Bond" or "Dream-Linked" traits. Creates surreal tension.

---

### 11: THE JOKER
**Core Instinct**: Deflection Through Humor

**Crisis Trigger**: Hopelessness, silence, fear of emotional truth

**Primary Response**: Uses sarcasm, jokes, or absurdity to shift mood

**Secondary Response**: Deflects responsibility, sabotages seriousness

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Inappropriate jokes during tragedy
- Pretends not to care—cracks when alone
- Breaks tension constantly, even when dangerous
- Uses humor to mask fear or guilt

**Spiral Path**: Becomes disruptive, mocks trauma, disrespects grief. May drive others away.

**Resilience Path**: Turns levity into survival tool—rallies morale when others break.

**Group Effect**:
- Lifts mood short term
- May erode gravity of decisions
- If broken, others often follow

**GM Use**: For characters who deflect with humor. Creates tension when jokes stop working.

---

### 12: THE WALL
**Core Instinct**: Hardened Shutdown

**Crisis Trigger**: Repeated emotional pain, overwhelming loss, prolonged stress

**Primary Response**: Shuts down all emotional display, becomes pure function

**Secondary Response**: Emotionless decisions, acts like machine

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Flat affect, monotone speech
- Refers to past traumas as irrelevant or "over"
- Volunteers for cold jobs (body disposal, mercy kills)
- Avoids intimacy or connection

**Spiral Path**: Becomes disconnected, may stop protecting others. Breaks only when alone.

**Resilience Path**: Becomes group's silent backbone—emotionless but loyal, enduring.

**Group Effect**:
- Seen as strong—until it's too late
- May discourage vulnerability in others
- Often viewed as either threat or anchor

**GM Use**: For characters who've endured too much. Creates tension through absence of emotion.

---

### 13: THE MIRROR
**Core Instinct**: Emotional Absorption

**Crisis Trigger**: Group collapse, interpersonal instability, survivor guilt

**Primary Response**: Reflects others' emotions intensely—becomes what surrounds them

**Secondary Response**: Internalizes group conflict, deteriorates rapidly

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Echoes group tone exactly—rage, silence, fear
- Shifts rapidly based on others' breakdowns
- Doesn't share independent feelings
- Becomes unstable around emotionally broken teammates

**Spiral Path**: Breaks when others do. Carries weight they shouldn't. May copy harmful behaviors.

**Resilience Path**: Becomes emotionally attuned leader—amplifies morale, protects hearts.

**Group Effect**:
- Highly sensitive to group state
- Can magnify chaos or unity
- Requires emotional balance from at least one other

**GM Use**: For empathic or emotionally sensitive characters. Creates feedback loops with group mood.

---

### 14: THE OUTSIDER
**Core Instinct**: Distrust and Distance

**Crisis Trigger**: Isolation, betrayal, or being overlooked

**Primary Response**: Emotionally withdraws and assumes worst intentions

**Secondary Response**: Starts acting as if no longer part of group

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Stops using "we" language
- Sleeps, eats, or guards alone
- Prepares solo escape plans
- Assumes every order or comment is a slight

**Spiral Path**: May defect, betray, or abandon others. Could pre-emptively sabotage trust.

**Resilience Path**: Can become fiercely loyal once truly accepted—but only once.

**Group Effect**:
- Destabilizes social cohesion
- Can "infect" group with suspicion
- NPCs may echo distrust if it grows

**GM Use**: For distrustful or isolated characters. Creates tension through paranoia and separation.

---

### 15: THE RECLAIMER
**Core Instinct**: Transformation Through Pain

**Crisis Trigger**: Personal breaking point—usually emotional, identity-based, or moral

**Primary Response**: Reflects inward. Begins redefining identity through action.

**Secondary Response**: Inspires others, reconstructs self through purpose

**Behavioral Signs**:
- Quiet self-reflection, purposeful ritual
- Sharp pivot in behavior ("That version of me died")
- Takes on new vow, mantra, or identity
- May symbolically reject old name or history

**Spiral Path**: If interrupted, loses clarity. May become erratic, spiritually numb, or zealously dangerous.

**Resilience Path**: Becomes beacon of new meaning. Radiates belief. Leads without demanding.

**Group Effect**:
- Deeply inspiring or unsettling
- May alienate old bonds while creating new ones
- Often becomes catalyst for group-wide change

**GM Use**: For characters undergoing transformation. Creates tension through radical change.

## USING CRISIS PROFILES

### Identification
1. **Observe character behavior under stress**
2. **Note response patterns across sessions**
3. **Match behavior to profile descriptions**
4. **Assign profile when pattern is clear**

### Application
- **Use behavioral signs** to guide NPC reactions
- **Frame scenes** that test or support profile
- **Track spiral vs resilience direction**
- **Create situations** where profile manifests

### Evolution
- **Profiles can shift** as characters change
- **Multiple profiles** may emerge at different times
- **Profiles deepen** through repeated activation
- **Profiles resolve** through breakthrough or Red Line moments

### Integration
- **Link to traits and flaws**: Profiles amplify existing character elements
- **Connect to Crisis Engine**: Profiles guide stress responses
- **Inform Red Lines**: Profiles suggest what boundaries matter most
- **Shape arcs**: Profiles provide direction for character development

## GM PRINCIPLES

### Don't Force Profiles
- Let them emerge naturally from play
- Watch for patterns, don't prescribe them
- If profile doesn't fit, don't use it
- Characters can surprise you

### Keep Profiles Secret (Usually)
- Don't tell players their profile unless asked
- Use profiles as GM guidance, not player restriction
- Let players discover their crisis behavior organically
- Reveal profiles only if it serves the story

### Support or Challenge
- Create scenes that test profile behavior
- Provide opportunities for resilience path
- Allow spiral if unaddressed
- Balance support and pressure

### Profiles Are Not Straitjackets
- Players can act against their profile
- Profiles describe tendencies, not absolutes
- Allow character growth beyond profile
- Let profiles evolve or fade

Crisis Profiles turn pressure into character definition—showing not who you are, but who you become.
