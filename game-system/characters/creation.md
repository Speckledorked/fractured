# Character Creation

## Overview

Character creation in Fractured is psychological and narrative-focused. Instead of choosing stats from a menu, you answer a 100-question quiz that reveals who your character is—their skills, weaknesses, fears, and breaking points.

## The Process

### Step 1: Complete the Quiz
Answer all 100 questions honestly, as your character would.
- Some questions are multiple choice
- Some are free response
- There are no wrong answers
- Your answers shape everything

### Step 2: GM Interprets Results
The GM uses your responses to assign:
- **8 stat values** (0-5 scale)
- **2-3 traits** (strengths and abilities)
- **1-2 flaws** (vulnerabilities and complications)
- **Crisis Profile** (behavior under stress)
- **Conflict fingerprint** (what drives and breaks you)

### Step 3: Declare Your Red Line
Choose one thing you will not do—your moral or emotional boundary.

Examples:
- "I won't kill innocents"
- "I won't abandon family"
- "I won't become like them"
- "I won't let others see me break"

### Step 4: Create Relationships
Establish connections to:
- Other player characters (how do you know them?)
- One NPC you care about
- One memory you can't let go

### Step 5: Final Touches
- Name your character
- Describe their appearance
- Note one item they carry from before
- Establish where they were when it started

## Stat Assignment Matrix

The GM assigns stats based on quiz patterns:

### Interpreting Responses

**0-1 points**: Weak, passive, detrimental, lacks experience
**2 points**: Average, functional with support
**3 points**: Confident, skilled, trained
**4 points**: Exceptional, hardened, professional
**5 points**: Peak capability (rare, earned through play)

### Distribution Guidelines

**Starting characters typically have:**
- Total of 16-20 points across all 8 stats
- No stat above 4 at creation
- At least one stat at 0-1 (weakness creates story)
- 2-3 stats at 3-4 (competence areas)
- Remaining stats at 2

### Cross-Reference Patterns

**Example 1: The Medic**
- Section A: Medical training (Q8) + helping skills (Q2)
- Section B: High empathy (Q27), steps in to help (Q35)
- Section C: Protects ethics (Q45), struggles with loss (Q58)
- Section D: Prioritizes worst-injured (Q72), gives second chances (Q73)

**Result:**
- Medical: 4
- Diplomacy: 3
- Sanity: 2
- Combat: 1
- Others: 2

**Example 2: The Survivor**
- Section A: Wilderness experience (Q7, Q14), improvisation (Q13)
- Section B: Lone operator (Q21), distrusts others (Q30)
- Section C: Survival sacrificed last (Q45), compartmentalizes (Q58)
- Section D: Acts on instinct (Q61-64), self-reliant (Q68)

**Result:**
- Survival: 4
- Stamina: 3
- Combat: 3
- Diplomacy: 1
- Others: 2

## Trait Selection

### Trait Categories
Choose traits from:
- **Core**: Consistent bonuses or advantages
- **Conditional**: Activate under specific circumstances
- **Passive**: Shape story without mechanical bonus
- **Latent**: Grow through play

### Assignment Process

1. **Identify Patterns**
   Look for repeated themes in responses:
   - Protective language → Protector traits
   - Analytical responses → Strategic traits
   - Emotional focus → Empathic traits
   - Practical focus → Resource traits

2. **Choose 2-3 Traits**
   - 1 Primary trait (strongly supported by quiz)
   - 1 Secondary trait (moderately supported)
   - 1 Optional situational trait

3. **Consider Balance**
   - Mix mechanical and narrative traits
   - Choose traits that create story opportunities
   - Avoid redundancy with high stats
   - Select traits that might evolve interestingly

### Example Selections

**For High Diplomacy Character:**
- Primary: "Trustworthy" (people believe you)
- Secondary: "Voice of Reason" (cuts through arguments)
- Situational: "Bridge-Builder" (connects opposites)

**For High Survival Character:**
- Primary: "Instinctive" (feels the right move)
- Secondary: "Efficient Forager" (finds what others miss)
- Situational: "Night-Eyed" (sees in dim light)

## Flaw Selection

### Flaw Categories
Choose flaws from:
- **Emotional**: Internal struggles
- **Behavioral**: Action patterns
- **Social**: Relationship issues
- **Physical**: Body limitations

### Assignment Process

1. **Identify Vulnerabilities**
   Look for:
   - Contradictions in responses
   - Emotional hot buttons
   - Social weak points
   - Physical limitations

2. **Choose 1-2 Flaws**
   - 1 Active flaw (triggers frequently)
   - 1 Optional passive flaw (lurks beneath)

3. **Make Them Meaningful**
   - Choose flaws that create dilemmas
   - Select flaws that might spiral
   - Pick flaws tied to Red Line potential
   - Avoid flaws that just annoy players

### Example Selections

**For Protective Character:**
- Active: "Self-Sacrificing" (puts others first always)
- Passive: "Savior Syndrome" (must save everyone)

**For Logical Character:**
- Active: "Detached" (keeps emotional distance)
- Passive: "Analysis Paralysis" (weighs every option too long)

## Conflict Profile Builder

Create a behavioral fingerprint with 5 elements:

### 1. Instinctive Response Pattern
How they handle stress/danger/decisions
Draw from Sections D & C

**Keywords**: Protect, Withdraw, Intervene, Control, Freeze, Analyze, Fight, Flee

### 2. Emotional Anchor
What belief, value, or feeling grounds them
Draw from Sections B, C & E

**Examples**:
- "My daughter is still out there"
- "Everyone deserves a second chance"
- "I keep people safe"
- "I won't repeat my past mistake"

### 3. Moral Threshold
Line they won't cross... yet
Draw from Section C

**Format**: "Will fracture if forced to _______"

**Examples**:
- "Kill innocents"
- "Abandon people behind"
- "Lie to protect myself"
- "Let others die for me"

### 4. Hidden Driver or Fear
What motivates or terrifies at their core
Draw from Section E (Q81-100)

**Examples**:
- "They lived, I didn't deserve to"
- "I'll be forgotten"
- "I'll lose control"
- "They abandoned me when I needed them most"

### 5. Crisis Persona (GM-Only)
Who they become when pushed

**Format**: Short evolving label

**Examples**:
- "The Unyielding Protector"
- "The Quiet Flame"
- "The Smiling Knife"
- "The Ghost with Guilt"
- "The One Who Runs"

This becomes their Crisis Profile guidance.

## Red Line Declaration

### What Is a Red Line?
A personal boundary—moral, emotional, or identity-based—that the character refuses to cross.

### How to Declare

**During Session Zero:**
Fill out the Red Line Declaration Sheet:

1. **Choose your line**: "I will never..."
   - Kill the innocent
   - Abandon the helpless
   - Become like my enemy
   - Let others die for me
   - Lie to protect myself
   - Hurt someone I love
   - Submit to cruelty
   - Lose control of who I am
   - Allow others to see me break
   - Forget who I used to be

2. **Explain why it matters**: 1-3 sentences about what happened before the collapse that made this line unbreakable

3. **State what might force you to cross it**:
   - To protect someone you love
   - To save many at the cost of one
   - Because there's no one else left to do it
   - Because if you don't, you'll die misunderstood

### Red Line Secrecy
- Give this to the GM privately
- The GM won't tell other players
- The GM won't force scenes around it immediately
- When the moment comes, it will matter

## Relationships

### To Other PCs
For each other player character, establish:
- **How you know them**: Before or during the collapse?
- **What you owe them**: Debt, protection, guilt, gratitude
- **What concerns you**: Their flaw, their risk-taking, their secret

### To One NPC
Create one NPC you care about:
- Name and relationship
- Where they are (with you, elsewhere, unknown)
- What they represent to you

### To One Memory
Choose one thing you can't let go:
- A promise you made
- Someone you lost
- Something you did
- Something that was done to you

These drive your arc.

## Final Touches

### Name
Choose a name that fits:
- Their origin
- Their identity
- How they see themselves now

### Appearance
Note:
- Age and build
- Distinguishing features
- How they carry themselves
- What their eyes show

### The Item
One physical item from before:
- A photo
- A letter
- A tool
- A symbol
- A weapon

Why do you still carry it?

### Where Were You?
When the outbreak started, where were you?
- At work
- At home
- On the road
- Somewhere that matters

How did you survive the first day?

## Integration with Play

### Session One
Introduce your character through:
- A moment of competence (showing high stat)
- A moment of weakness (showing low stat or flaw)
- A connection to another PC
- A hint of your Red Line

### Ongoing Play
Your character sheet evolves:
- Stats increase through Growth Marks
- Traits evolve through crisis
- Flaws spiral or breakthrough
- Relationships deepen or fracture
- Red Line approaches

### By Campaign End
Your character will be transformed:
- Different stats (earned through experience)
- Evolved traits (spiral or resilience paths)
- Resolved or deepened flaws
- Red Line crossed or held
- Relationships lost, gained, or forever changed

You will not be who you were when this started.

That's the point.
