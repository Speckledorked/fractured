# Red Lines

## Overview

A **Red Line** is the moral, emotional, or identity boundary a character refuses to cross—until they must. It's the personal rule that defines who they are, and crossing it permanently changes them.

## Purpose

Red Lines:
- Define character identity at deepest level
- Create high-stakes moral dilemmas
- Enable transformative character moments
- Provide GM with powerful dramatic tools
- Ensure characters face their defining choices

## What Is a Red Line?

A Red Line is:
- **Personal**: Unique to each character
- **Absolute**: "I will never..." (until the moment)
- **Identity-defining**: Crossing it changes who they are
- **Rare**: Reserved for campaign-defining moments
- **Permanent**: Once crossed, can't be undone

A Red Line is NOT:
- A minor preference
- Something easily broken
- A tactical choice
- A group rule
- A mechanical restriction

## The Six Tags

Red Lines fall into six categories:

### [🧍 Identity]
**"If I do this, I'm not me anymore"**

The line protects core identity or self-definition.

**Examples**:
- "I won't become like my father"
- "I won't lose control of who I am"
- "I won't let others see me break"
- "I won't forget who I used to be"

### [💔 Loyalty]
**"If I do this, I betray who I care about"**

The line protects relationships or commitments.

**Examples**:
- "I won't abandon family"
- "I won't let others die for me"
- "I won't leave people behind"
- "I won't betray their trust"

### [⚖️ Morality]
**"If I do this, I become the monster"**

The line protects ethical boundaries.

**Examples**:
- "I won't kill innocents"
- "I won't torture"
- "I won't lie to save myself"
- "I won't let cruelty stand"

### [🕊 Humanity]
**"If I do this, I stop being human"**

The line protects what makes them person vs animal/monster.

**Examples**:
- "I won't eat human flesh"
- "I won't kill for pleasure"
- "I won't abandon my compassion"
- "I won't lose my capacity to care"

### [⛓ Control]
**"If I do this, I prove I have no choice left"**

The line protects agency and freedom.

**Examples**:
- "I won't submit to tyranny"
- "I won't be controlled by fear"
- "I won't let them make me a weapon"
- "I won't obey orders that contradict my conscience"

### [🪞 Belief]
**"If I do this, I was wrong about everything"**

The line protects worldview or faith.

**Examples**:
- "I won't accept that people can't change"
- "I won't believe survival requires becoming evil"
- "I won't give up on hope"
- "I won't let the world make me cynical"

## Red Line Scenes

### Scene Structure (5 Steps)

**Step 1: Identify the Line**
What will they not do—even if it costs them everything?

Use player's declaration or infer from behavior.

**Step 2: Invert the Stakes**
Why does breaking the Red Line seem like the only way to save something greater?

Make the "wrong" choice feel justified—even heroic.

**Examples**:
- Killing one traitor saves dozens
- Leaving someone behind protects the group
- Becoming the monster prevents worse horrors
- Lying saves lives

**Step 3: Deny Easy Outs**
Remove third options. It must be yes or no. Now or never. You or them.

- Limit time
- Remove support
- Make only one PC capable of acting
- Let world move faster than intervention

**Step 4: Frame the Moment**
Use short, sensory detail. Let emotion rise. Then:

**Ask the character, not the player.**

> "He's looking at you, whispering your name. Do you shut the door?"

Then—stop narrating. Let the table breathe.

**Step 5: Echo the Aftermath**
What changes—even if they "succeeded"?

Choose one or more:
- NPC relationship altered forever
- Morale or reputation penalty
- PC gains new trait or flaw
- Something they say later no longer sounds true
- Group sees them differently
- They see themselves differently

Let this moment be part of them—even if no one talks about it.

### Example Scene: "Blood for Shelter"

**Tags**: [⚖️ Morality] [💔 Loyalty]

**Setup**:
Group is offered shelter—but only if one person is left outside.

**Red Line**: "I won't abandon people"

**Inversion**:
Leaving one saves four. Who decides? Who volunteers? Who chooses?

**Denial of Outs**:
- Time limit: Decide in 30 seconds
- NPC won't negotiate
- Only four can fit
- Someone will die if left outside

**The Moment**:
> "The door is closing. [Name] is still outside. They're looking at you. You have your hand on the door handle. What do you do?"

**Aftermath**:
- If shut door: [Name] survives alone or dies. PC gains "Survivor's Guilt" flaw or evolves toward "Pragmatist" trait.
- If refuse: Group stays outside together. NPC closes door. New crisis emerges.
- If volunteer to stay: Heroic, but may not survive. Group forever changed by sacrifice.

## Red Line Prompt Library

### 10 Ready-to-Use Scenarios

**1. "He Begged Me Not To"**
Tags: [💔 Loyalty] [🪞 Belief]

Friend begs PC not to kill infected loved one. Others demand it.
- Infected stirs—fast, lethal, worsening
- Choice: Spare for friend or end it fast
- Echo: Friend may never speak again. Or thanks them too late.

**2. "It Sounds Like Her"**
Tags: [🧍 Identity] [🕊 Humanity]

Trapped infected mimics loved one's voice.
- PC hears child, spouse, or parent calling from dark
- Choice: Open door or walk away forever
- Echo: Voice disappears. Later, new voice emerges speaking their name.

**3. "One Shot. Not Both."**
Tags: [⚖️ Morality] [⛓ Control]

Two allies trapped. PC has one bullet.
- Debris collapse, fire closing in. One can be saved.
- Choice: Choose who dies. And who knows it.
- Echo: Saved survivor may not forgive. Or pretends they would've done same.

**4. "The Cure Needs a Killer"**
Tags: [🪞 Belief] [🧍 Identity]

Life-saving treatment can be made—but only with blood from unwilling innocent person.
- Medical expert: "We can save dozens. But they'll die."
- Choice: Take the blood or let many die
- Echo: May gain cure. But cost follows player always.

**5. "The Child Saw Everything"**
Tags: [🕊 Humanity] [💔 Loyalty]

Child NPC witnessed group do something horrific.
- Child begins telling others. Tensions rise.
- Choice: Silence them or let truth unravel group
- Echo: Trauma echoes in every trust check. They might still talk... eventually.

**6. "Give Up the Ghost"**
Tags: [🧍 Identity] [🪞 Belief]

Player ordered to destroy something symbolic: journal, ring, dog tag.
- Threat: "If they track you, it'll get us killed."
- Choice: Burn the symbol or risk everyone else
- Echo: Loss of stability. Later, hallucination—or rediscovery—of what was lost.

**7. "The Ones Who Starved"**
Tags: [⚖️ Morality] [⛓ Control]

Only remaining food belonged to another group... who are now dead.
- Known rule: "We never take from the dead"
- Choice: Feed your people or keep the rule
- Echo: Rulebreak opens floodgate. NPCs begin taking more than food.

**8. "He Could Still Come Back"**
Tags: [💔 Loyalty] [🪞 Belief]

Bitten PC is tied up. Others ask you to watch them—just in case.
- They sleep. You hold weapon.
- Choice: Kill them now or risk them waking changed
- Echo: If they wake up, they remember everything.

**9. "The Mercy Window"**
Tags: [⚖️ Morality] [🕊 Humanity]

Stranger is dying—begging for quick end. Rest of party refuses. PC is alone with them for 10 seconds.
- Choice: Grant mercy or let them suffer
- Echo: Either choice echoes later. No judgment—just weight.

**10. "The Red Line Is Yours"**
Tags: [Wildcard]

GM takes player's actual Red Line declaration—and builds scene to break it.
- Personalized scene. Built in silence. Dropped without warning.
- Choice: The one they swore they'd never make
- Echo: Entire arc shifts. New trait or flaw earned. Nothing scripted—only felt.

## GM Guidelines

### Preparation
**Before Using Red Lines:**
- Have player declarations on file
- Know each character's moral boundaries
- Wait for dramatically appropriate moment
- Build toward it, don't spring it randomly

### Execution
**During Red Line Scene:**
- Set the scene clearly
- State the stakes simply
- Remove alternatives explicitly
- Ask the character (not meta-discussion)
- Allow silence after the question
- Honor whatever they choose

### Aftermath
**After Red Line Scene:**
- Don't rush to next scene
- Let the weight settle
- Show reactions from NPCs/PCs
- Apply lasting consequences
- Don't punish the choice—show its cost
- Let character reflect later

### Frequency
**How Often:**
- Once per major arc (3-5 sessions)
- Once per Season in campaign
- Maximum 1-2 times per character per campaign
- Save for moments that matter most

Red Lines should be rare enough to shock, powerful enough to transform.

### Integration
**Red Lines Connect To:**
- **Crisis Profiles**: Often trigger or test profiles
- **Trait Evolution**: May cause trait spiral or resilience
- **Flaw Breakthrough**: Can force flaw confrontation
- **Growth Marks**: Major Red Line moments may grant bonus marks
- **Relationships**: Permanently alter how others see character

## Example: Complete Red Line Scene

**Character**: Maya, former EMT with high Medical, low Combat
**Red Line**: [💔 Loyalty] "I won't let others die for me"
**Crisis Profile**: The Martyr
**Current Situation**: Group trapped, surrounded by infected

### Setup
> "The ventilation shaft is barely wide enough for one person. It's the only way out. Marcus is hurt—he won't make it through fast enough. The infected are breaking through the barricade. You have maybe thirty seconds."

### Stakes
Someone has to stay behind to buy time. Who?

### Denial
- Time limit: 30 seconds
- Shaft too narrow for carrying someone
- Marcus can't move fast due to injury
- No weapons left except one knife
- Infected will reach you before everyone gets through

### The Moment
> "Marcus is pushing you toward the shaft. 'Go,' he says. 'Get them out.' The barricade splinters. You see his face—he knows he won't make it. He's choosing this. But you can stay instead. What do you do?"

### Player Choice
- **Goes through**: Lets Marcus sacrifice himself. Lives with it.
- **Stays behind**: Violates "won't let others die for me" but prevents Marcus's death. May not survive.
- **Forces Marcus through**: Uses authority/strength to make him go first. Creates different kind of guilt.

### Aftermath (If She Goes)
- Marcus doesn't make it out
- Group survives because of his sacrifice
- Maya gains "Survivor's Guilt" flaw or "Martyr" profile intensifies
- Later scene: She finds Marcus's medical kit. Has to decide whether to use it.
- Relationship change: One NPC says "You made the right call." Another won't look at her.

### Long-term Echo
- Every medical decision now carries weight
- When she saves someone: "Marcus should be here"
- When she loses someone: "I survived for this?"
- May evolve into breakthrough moment or deeper spiral

## Troubleshooting

### "Player Won't Decide"
- Respect the freeze—it's powerful roleplay
- Have environment force decision (infected break through)
- Let another PC intervene
- Make indecision itself a choice with consequences

### "Player Finds Third Option"
- If creative and earned: allow it
- If avoiding the moment: gently close the loophole
- Remember: point is transformation, not punishment
- Sometimes clever solutions should work

### "Wrong Timing"
- If scene falls flat, don't force it
- Cut scene early if players aren't engaged
- Save Red Line for better moment
- Not every session needs Red Line moment

### "Too Dark for Table"
- Know your table's boundaries
- Use safety tools (X-card, lines/veils)
- Check in before heavy scenes
- Fade to black if needed
- Debrief after intense moments

### "Player Ignores Red Line"
- That's their choice—honor it
- Character has changed—reflect that
- May not actually be their Red Line
- Discover what their real line is

## Red Line Philosophy

Red Lines are invitations to become someone different.

They ask:
- **Who are you when everything is at stake?**
- **What makes you you?**
- **How much pressure before you break?**
- **Can you live with who you become?**

They don't exist to punish.
They exist to transform.

Use them wisely.
Use them sparingly.
Use them when they matter.

When a Red Line breaks, a character is reborn—for better or worse.
