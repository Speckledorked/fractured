🎣 SCENE HOOK LIBRARY – SOLO CHARACTER PROMPTS 

Each hook includes:

Scene Seed


Underlying Moral/Emotional Pressure


How to Use (GM Tips)


1. “The Pack Slows Down for One”

Scene: A player’s character is injured. The group is being pursued.

Tension: Are they worth the risk?

GM Tip: Don’t ask “Do they leave them?” Ask: Who volunteers to stay behind? Who speaks first?

2. “What You Bury Doesn’t Stay Buried”

Scene: The player discovers a personal item—something they thought was lost with their family, unit, or home.

Tension: What if the past is still alive—or watching?

GM Tip: No exposition needed. Just leave the item. Let them react. Then start the consequences.

3. “The Mercy Window”

Scene: A stranger is dying—begging for a quick end. The rest of the party refuses. The PC is alone with them for 10 seconds.

Tension: What kind of mercy is moral?

GM Tip: Don’t punish either decision. Make both have narrative weight. Let it echo later.

4. “The Mask Slips”

Scene: During a night watch or casual moment, another character says something that echoes a trauma or secret. The PC reacts—visibly.

Tension: Do they shut down, lash out, confess—or lie again?

GM Tip: Use a natural conversation. Let the scene breathe. Let silence say more than words.

5. “You Were Supposed to Save Them”

Scene: A survivor the PC was tasked with protecting dies. Others blame them.

Tension: Was it their fault—or just their responsibility?

GM Tip: Run it after success—during celebration or rest. Let the accusation cut into their calm.

🔥 What Makes These Work?

They offer no “right” answer


They don’t require mechanics—just framing


They let players reveal themselves through action


🎣 SCENE HOOK LIBRARY – PARTY CONFLICT SEEDS

Each includes:

Scene Prompt


Conflict Pressure Point


GM Cue for Escalation


6. “You Lied to Keep the Peace”

Scene: One player withheld a truth: contaminated food, a non-lethal bite, a deal with hostile survivors. The lie worked—but it’s exposed.

Tension: Was the lie justified? Or betrayal?

GM Cue: Let NPCs agree with both sides. The group fractures around the fallout.

7. “Someone Always Eats First”

Scene: Rations are short. One player’s been getting more (due to injury, skill, status). Now it’s public.

Tension: Who decides worth?

GM Cue: Don’t script the fight. Just present the imbalance. Watch where blame lands.

8. “The Risk Vote”

Scene: The group must decide: split up, sacrifice one, or abandon a mission. It’s a vote—and one PC abstains.

Tension: Is neutrality safety? Cowardice? Guilt?

GM Cue: Make consequences real. Not voting still has weight. Let others push them.

9. “The Unburied Dead”

Scene: A corpse from the group’s past is discovered again—one that wasn’t given proper rest.

Tension: Whose job was it to let them go?

GM Cue: Let the body carry symbols (worn scarf, half-note, marked map). Let grief clash with practicality.

10. “One of You Is Lying”

Scene: A survivor is dead. The story doesn’t match. Only the PCs were there.

Tension: Who’s protecting who—and from what?

GM Cue: Don’t resolve it. Let doubt fester. Let it alter how players position themselves around each other.

🔥 How to Use These

Drop them organically—not as “quests,” but as emotional ruptures


Let NPCs absorb fallout too (trust, fear, recruitment, or revolt)


Don’t seek closure—seek change


🎣 SCENE HOOK LIBRARY – EXTERNAL THREAT TENSION SEEDS

11. “The Screamer in the Field”

Scene: A wounded person is calling for help. They’re trapped—but also drawing in infected. Saving them puts everyone at risk.

Tension: What are you willing to risk for a stranger?

GM Cue: Play the screams in real time. Time matters. Watch who breaks first.

12. “The Rescue Is a Trap”

Scene: A survivor radios for help—but when the group arrives, they realize it’s bait. The caller isn’t a villain—they’re just hungry.

Tension: What do you do with someone who became what you’re afraid to?

GM Cue: Let the baiter explain why. Let it make sense. Then watch the group fracture on what to do next.

13. “The Map with No Return”

Scene: A found map marks a location with heavy supplies—but it’s marked with an X and the word “DON’T.”

Tension: Trust the warning? Or chase survival?

GM Cue: Let the risk feel legendary. Build internal disagreement. Let greed or fear set the tone.

14. “The Child Has a Knife”

Scene: A lone child is found—wild-eyed, silent. Armed. They won’t speak. They won’t lower the weapon.

Tension: Who sees a threat? Who sees a victim? Who hesitates?

GM Cue: Play up silence. One second of panic could cost everything—or reveal everything.

15. “The Thing That Watches”

Scene: Every night, someone reports seeing movement. Not infected. Not human. Just… watching.

Tension: What happens when fear becomes shape?

GM Cue: Use it to slowly escalate paranoia. Let the fear turn inward—until someone takes action, right or wrong.

🧠 Why These Work

They challenge:

Trust in strangers


Willingness to kill or abandon


What “threat” even means in a dying world


And best of all: you never have to answer the question. Let the players do that.

🧠 Red Line Builder: Core Principles

🔺 What is a Red Line?

A Red Line is the moral, emotional, or identity boundary a character refuses to cross—until they must.

It might be:

“I won’t kill the innocent.”


“I protect children no matter what.”


“I never leave family behind.”


“I will never become like them.”


Crossing it changes them forever.

🧰 Red Line Scene Structure

Every Red Line scene has 4 parts:

🔪 Scene Tags (for Red Line Hooks)

Each scene will be tagged by what kind of boundary it targets:

[🧍‍♂️ Identity] – “If I do this, I’m not me anymore.”


[💔 Loyalty] – “If I do this, I betray who I care about.”


[⚖️ Morality] – “If I do this, I become the monster.”


[🕊 Humanity] – “If I do this, I stop being human.”


[⛓ Control] – “If I do this, I prove I have no choice left.”


[🪞 Belief] – “If I do this, I was wrong about everything.”


🔥 Example: Red Line Format

🪓 Scene: “The Screaming Door”

Tag: [💔 Loyalty]

Setup: An NPC the PC cares about is locked in a room with infected approaching. The only way to stop the door from being opened is to shoot them now—fast, clean, and certain.

Red Line: “I won’t kill people I love.”

Pressure Inversion: If they don’t—everyone else may die.

Echo Options:

They pull the trigger: survivor blames them forever


They hesitate: someone else dies and blames them


They walk away: the door opens. The cost is shared.

🪓 Step-by-Step Build Structure

1. Identify the Red Line

What will they not do—even if it costs them everything?

📌 This should come from player behavior, trait choices, or past dialogue.

🧠 Examples:

“I never abandon people.”


“I’ll never kill a child.”


“I won’t become like my father.”


“I don’t torture. I don’t lie.”


2. Invert the Stakes

Why does breaking the Red Line seem like the only way to save something greater?

Design a scene where the “wrong” choice feels justified—even heroic.

🧨 Examples:

Killing one traitor saves dozens.


Leaving someone behind protects the group.


Becoming the monster prevents worse horrors.


3. Deny the Easy Outs

Remove third options. It must be yes or no. Now or never. You or them.

Limit time, remove support, and make only one PC capable of acting.

🧠 If other players try to intervene, let the world move faster than them.

4. Frame the Moment

Use short, sensory detail. Let emotion rise. And then:

🎭 Ask the character, not the player.

“He’s looking at you, whispering your name. Do you shut the door?”

Then—stop narrating. Let the table breathe.

5. Echo the Aftermath

What changes—even if they “succeeded”?

Choose one or more:

NPC relationship altered forever


Morale or reputation penalty


The PC gains a new trait or flaw


Something they say later no longer sounds true


Let this moment be part of them—even if no one else talks about it.

🧠 Optional Layer: GM Pressure Prompts

Use these questions when designing a Red Line moment:

What does this cost the PC that no one else sees?


Who else wanted them to break it—or needed them to hold?


What if they don’t break it—and someone else does?

“It Sounds Like Her”

Conflict: A trapped infected mimics a loved one’s voice.

Trigger: The PC hears their child, spouse, or parent calling from the dark.

Choice: Open the door—or walk away forever.

Echo: Voice disappears. Later, a new voice emerges… speaking their name.

4. 

“One Shot. Not Both.”

Tags: [⚖️ Morality] [⛓ Control]

Conflict: Two allies are trapped. The PC has one bullet.

Trigger: Debris collapse, fire closing in. One can be saved.

Echo: Saved survivor may not forgive. Or may pretend they would’ve done the same.

5. 

“The Cure Needs a Killer”

Tags: [🪞 Belief] [🧍 Identity]

Conflict: A life-saving treatment can be made—but only with blood from an unwilling, innocent person.

Trigger: Medical expert says: “We can save dozens. But they’ll die.”

Choice: Take the blood… or let many die.

Echo: May gain a cure. But the cost follows the player home—always.

6. 

“The Child Saw Everything”

Tags: [🕊 Humanity] [💔 Loyalty]

Conflict: A child NPC witnessed the group do something horrific.

Trigger: The child begins telling others. Tensions rise.

Choice: Silence them—or let the truth unravel the group.

Echo: Trauma echoes in every trust check. They might still talk… eventually.

7. 

“Give Up the Ghost”

Tags: [🧍 Identity] [🪞 Belief]

Conflict: The player is ordered to destroy something symbolic: an old journal, wedding ring, dog tag.

Trigger: Threats rise: “If they track you, it’ll get us killed.”

Choice: Burn the symbol… or risk everyone else.

Echo: Loss of stability. Later, hallucination—or rediscovery—of what was lost.

8. 

“The Ones Who Starved”

Tags: [⚖️ Morality] [⛓ Control]

Conflict: The only remaining food belonged to another group… who are now dead.

Trigger: A known rule: “We never take from the dead.”

Choice: Feed your people—or keep the rule.

Echo: Rulebreak opens floodgate. NPCs begin taking more than just food.

9. 

“He Could Still Come Back”

Tags: [💔 Loyalty] [🪞 Belief]

Conflict: A bitten PC is tied up by the others. They ask you to watch them—just in case.

Trigger: They sleep. You hold the weapon.

Choice: Kill them now—or risk them waking up… changed.

Echo: If they wake up, they remember everything.

10. 

“The Red Line Is Yours”

Tags: [Wildcard]

Conflict: The GM takes the player’s actual Red Line declaration from Session Zero—and builds a scene to break it.

Trigger: Personalized scene. Built in silence. Dropped without warning.

Choice: The one they swore they’d never make.

Echo: Entire arc shifts. New trait or flaw earned. Nothing scripted—only felt.

Step | Description

1. Establish the Line | Something the player or their character has previously expressed or shown. Ideally seeded by past play.

2. Invert the Stakes | Frame the situation so not crossing the line causes harm—to others, to the group, to the world.

3. Force the Moment | No time to vote. No third option. The decision has to be made now, and only they can make it.

4. Let It Echo | Whatever they choose—let it have weight. Not punishment. Just change. They should never look at themselves the same again.
